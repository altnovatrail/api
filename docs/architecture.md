## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does:**
- works as the only service of the first slice. `tutor`, `module-kit`, `learner-model` and `engine` are libraries in its process, and `tutor` becomes a service only when it has a second consumer;
- checks the token of the sign-in service and the permissions: which of the account owners acts for which learner. A child learns under an adult's account, an adult under their own, and this may be one account: it holds both the profile of the owner themselves as a learner and the profiles of their children. The sign-in service is Firebase Auth, through Google alone, and sign-in through Apple waits on Т-41; the permissions are OpenFGA in the process of `api`;
- keeps the learners and their consent:
  - the profile: the pseudonym, the avatar, the age category, the country and the date when the age was given, the presentation style — it includes whether voicing is on and its speed —, the default solving mode — "by steps" or "self" —, the hint mode — "questions only" or "show where", by default "show where" —, the interface language, which an adult sets, a BCP 47 tag, and the grade from 1 to 12 — if a module asks for it: the module manifest names the grades. There are no interests until the format gives a task a presentation by them. The solving mode is a field of its own, not part of the presentation style, and of the presentation style only what changes the words goes to AI. There is neither a date of birth nor an age in years;
  - the consent records: who gave it for the learner, when and how it was checked;
- keeps the attempts:
  - pins to an attempt the digest of its module package — the digest of its inventory —, the module format version, the version of `module-kit` and the seed. It takes the seed from a cryptographically secure source when the attempt begins, and does not give it to the device: the platform's generator is not cryptographically secure, and the seed can be seen from the state of a place of randomness;
  - gives a task only to a learner of its age categories, `age_categories`, and a lesson only if both the lesson and each of its tasks suit the learner, and checks this every time it gives a lesson and begins the attempt of one of its segments;
  - takes the instance of a task template by the attempt's seed from the numbers that the package carries, and substitutes them by the code of `module-kit`: Starlark is not run on an attempt;
  - gives the app the task's open view, and the key, the task's solution, the traps, the techniques, the slot checks, the skills, the difficulty band and the difficulty stay on the server. The open views are built by `module-kit` from the server part: the manifest, the skill graph, the technique catalog and the solving steps `api` gives by the digest of the attempt's package, a lesson's to a learner whom it suits, a task's with an attempt;
- leads lessons: gives a lesson's open view — without skills and age categories — and the lesson's tasks by its segments. A lesson's task is the same attempt as outside a lesson. `api` leads a lesson by a lesson run — a row per profile and lesson, on the package on which the lesson began, one for all devices —, and it ends with "lesson passed" from `app`, when all the task segments have ended, with "unchecked", "not suitable" or "abandoned" after 30 days without actions. The place among the explanations and examples and the state of showing are held by `app`;
- checks the answer:
  - handwriting: a submission, `tutor` reads the work, an observation results, `module-kit` gives a verdict on each answer slot and on the item. Reading and analysis are two calls: the model sees the key and the task's solution only in the analysis. Handwriting is read by two readers of one provider, `module-kit` joins their observations into one, and an answer slot that they read differently is "unreadable"; or, in the mode `single` the deployment names, by Claude Haiku 5.5 at `medium` alone, whose one observation `module-kit` checks and gives the verdict on. `api` stores the joined observation, or the one reader's, `novatrail.observation.v1` and beside it the tapped choice, without writing parts and other readings: by them the verdict is repeated without AI. Nothing of the writing history goes to AI;
  - the predicates of the slot checks `api` computes by the code of `module-kit` in the request's flow and no more than `GOMAXPROCS` at once: the clock of `module-kit` is 1 s per predicate by wall time, and "unchecked" must not depend on the load. A cancellation of the request's `context` is an error without a verdict, not "unchecked": the submission stays without a verdict. The dialect of Starlark and the version of go.starlark.net are part of the format, and the predicates of third-party modules from stage 4 go in a separate process with a memory limit (О-9);
  - the choice of an option by tapping is without AI: `module-kit` compares what was chosen with the slot check. An option written by hand is read by `tutor`, like any handwriting. There is no keyboard input, editing or widget state in format v0;
  - a task with a rubric: the reading of the essay and the assessment by the rubric in `tutor` and feedback by the rubric, without a verdict. A submission ends with feedback, "unreadable" or "unchecked", the attempt is open for rewriting, and the learner's "Done" closes it. Of such an attempt the server stores the criteria's marks, without the text of the essay and the words of the feedback — the recommendation of `api` for П-19;
- by the result of an attempt, not of a separate submission, gives evidence to `learner-model`, and `engine` chooses the next lesson or task and writes the reason code. Inside a lesson the next thing is its segment, and the engine is called when the lesson run has ended. The list of reason codes is a contract of `spec`. The choice stores the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the digests of the packages and a mark of which of them are the engine's modules, the reason code, the release of `modules`, the number of the last evidence, the skill chosen on the skill map, the age category of the profile at the time of the choice and whether the pool was limited to tasks without AI, so that it can be repeated by the evidence log without AI; the seed field is empty while the engine takes no randomness. Beyond the learner model the engine gets from `api` the summary of issues, collected from the records of the choice and of attempts, and the modules, the lessons and the pool of tasks `api` has already selected by age category: the engine's input does not have it;
- transfers progress after a major version of a module at every computation of the state by the log: `learner-model` maps the evidence by `retired_skills` of the current version's graph, and the log does not change. Evidence about a skill without a counterpart goes nowhere, and evidence about a skill that the current version's graph does not know waits in the log until the next version names it;
- leads the hint ladder: the program chooses the rung by the number of wrong answers, and from the fourth wrong answer offers to call an adult or to show the solution (О-7). To a child the solution is shown only after the ladder, and while the adult has not turned off the setting "ask before showing", by an adult action; showing closes the attempt. The model's question and the fallback text speak about the first wrong answer slot in order. `api` gives the fallback text when the model's question did not come or did not pass the checks: at rungs 1–2, the text of the trap value, the trap advice or the module's rung template, and at the third, the item's simpler case;
- gives the task's technique — at the solving step whose `gives` gives it, and at the learner's request before the first wrong answer, without AI and without reading the work — and records which technique it gave, at a step or on request. "Givens and unknown" are open: `app` shows them from the task's open view, and `api` does not record them. After a wrong answer the request gives the current rung once more, and "explain differently" gives the same rung in other words, with one call to AI per rung, without the work (О-21). It sends no unsent work for reading, neither on request nor on a pause. The request before the first wrong answer also gives the task's closed leading question, if it has one: in the contract, `GetHintResponse.leading_question`, and for an open attempt, `OpenAttempt.requested_leading_question`, and it is empty both for a task without a question and beside the rung's words. `api` records that it gave the question as a technique on request, and the question goes into the evidence only by its own field of `learner-model`, `leading_question_on_request`;
- checks for leaks any text of the tutor before it goes to the device, so the tutor's words go whole, not as a stream. The leak check itself is the code of `module-kit`, and how it searches is set normatively by the text of the module format, [spec/format/README.md](../spec/format/README.md);
- there too checks the form of a hint and the feedback rule of a rubric, by the code of `tutor`, the same as in its eval. A hint that did not pass the check is the fallback text, and instead of feedback there is the phrase "no feedback this time";
- accepts an adult's problem report, "report a problem", which `app` offers at each answer of the tutor, and passes it to the operator, without the content of the work beyond what the adult attached themselves. It is `AttemptService.ReportProblem`, with an idempotency key, and the operator reviews it within the period `store.ReviewWithin` sets, by `api reports`, and acts by the escalation path;
- counts the platform AI quotas by the spending that `tutor` reports: in dollars by its estimate, per family day, with a profile cap and an account cap, which the account's tariff sets, and the account gets the tariff with an invite code. An exhausted quota closes platform AI until the next day, and an own key does not spend the quota. The tariffs of stage 3 are О-14, payment comes from stage 3;
- gives its part of the export — `app` builds the export — and deletes the data from the database and the account at the sign-in service, when the deletion is confirmed by a fresh sign-in. A fresh sign-in is `auth_time` no older than 5 minutes. A profile is deleted separately from the account, and an account by the mark "being deleted" in the same transaction as its data, then by the tuples of OpenFGA and by the user at the sign-in service; what did not go through, an operator's job finishes. To other devices `api` answers "account gone", and of a child consent a minimal record remains;
- carries the operator's jobs and commands, like `admin` in canvas-api, and not a separate app: migrations — of the OpenFGA store too —, retention periods (П-9), and with them finishing deletions and deleting users of the sign-in service without an account, the consent check — the operator checks the signed form and keeps its scan outside the database of `api` —, access to the closed slice by invite codes and the change of an account's tariff, opening platform AI after "platform limit", the list of countries before the DPIA, the cleanup of "dangling" tuples and of `changelog` of OpenFGA, and the job after a deployment, which closes the attempts on packages that left the image;
- in the first slice holds the module packages in its image and serves the files of their device parts — media files, `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` (Т-17):
  - a pull request pins in the configuration of `api` — the file `modules.json` — the release of `modules` and the digest of its index and serves as the trust anchor, and CI of each pull request checks the index's signature by the recipe `release-verify` of `spec`. From then on the chain of digests from the pinned index holds the packages: index → inventory → parts → files;
  - gives a new attempt on the current version of a module — the newest by semver version of the pinned index that the index has not marked withdrawn and whose format version is in the window of `api` —, and a lesson's task on the package on which the lesson began. A withdrawn version stays in the image only for repeating a verdict: the attempts begun on it are closed "unchecked", and `api` no longer gives either the files of its device part or its open views;
  - the image holds all the versions of the pinned index whose format version is in the window — and so all the versions in the window to which unfinished attempts are pinned; for repeating a verdict outside the window, old versions are taken from the releases of `modules`. A package whose module format version has left the window leaves the image, and the attempts on it are closed "unchecked" by the job after a deployment, and before it by the check at a request; repeating its verdict goes outside `api`, by the release of `module-kit` that checked it.

**Does not:**
- does not call AI providers bypassing `tutor`. CI of `api` checks the full list of dependencies: the SDKs of AI providers enter the build only through `tutor`;
- does not pass to `tutor` the identifiers of the learner and of the account, the pseudonym and the adult's data — except the own key: the request types of `tutor` have no fields for them;
- does not decide for the libraries: `module-kit` gives the verdict, `learner-model` computes the learner model, `engine` chooses what is next, and AI only reads the work and chooses the words. `learner-model` and `engine` read neither packages nor the database: `api` passes them the graph and the evidence. `api` turns the graph from a module package into one form — into the types that `learner-model` sets, and `engine` takes from it —, with the anchor skills of the shared taxonomy and the field `teaches`;
- does not store the picture or crop of the work and the sound that go to checking, or the own key: they live only in the request's memory. `app` sends neither strokes nor a whole photo to the server, but for a stored work while the storage of the profile's works is on. A stored work — the history of writing, the context of each submission and the picture of the strokes, as the device holds them, of a handwriting, an essay or a drawing — is kept only while whoever consents for the learner keeps the storage on, and by default it is off. Of an essay it stores neither the read text, nor the justifications, nor the words of the feedback: they live in the request's memory and on the device;
- does not write into logs direct identifiers, IP addresses, works, what was read and the text of hints. The request logs of Cloud Run are excluded in Cloud Logging, and the region of the logs is `us-east4`, as for the whole platform;
- sends no letters or notifications: the adult's contacts are needed only for sign-in and the subscription;
- keeps no real-time channel: the platform's architecture of the first slice has none, and the check of handwriting is two requests, the submission with the verdict and then the tutor's words. This is the recommendation of `api` for Т-25, and the channel is to be returned to together with voice.

**Data and links:**
- the database of `api` is the only store of learners' data on the server among the parts of the platform. Which database it is, `api` itself decides: Neon Postgres, as in canvas-api. Beside it, in the same Neon project, is the second database of `api`, the OpenFGA store with the tuples "account — profile". The export, deletion and backups cover both;
- the database holds the account, the learners' profiles, consent, attempts and observations, the learner model, and the stored works only while their storage is on for the profile. An essay has no observation: of its attempt the database keeps only the criteria's marks. The learner model lives in the database of `api` — the recommendation of `api` for П-7 —, and it is stored as an append-only evidence log, where the state of the skills is a cache — the recommendation for Т-22. Backups hold the same as the database, until the end of their period (П-9): for Neon it is the history window;
- outside the platform part of the data is held by the sign-in service — the account owner's sign-in method; it is Firebase Auth, and it processes the data only in the USA (sources 2.1) —, by the AI providers — only what is needed for one call —, and, from stage 3, by payment;
- the client of `api` in the first slice is `app`, the app of the learner and the adult. `skill-map` draws the skill map from ready data from `api`, which `app` passes to it. `api` itself calls the sign-in service and its database, the AI providers only through `tutor`, and from stage 3 payment;
- at build time depends on `tutor`, `module-kit`, `learner-model`, `engine` and `spec`. The code of `spec` it takes as the Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec` at the tag of the submodule `spec/`: the format and its types are the packages `types/formatv0` and `types/formatv0/open`, and `api` reads the open view with them, not with copies of its own. The module's version in `go.mod` and the submodule are moved together, and the module `spec` is excluded from Dependabot `gomod` — and so is the contracts module below, otherwise Dependabot would raise it separately from the submodule. That they stand at the submodule's tag, `ci-lint` checks. The API contracts are proto3 files in `contracts/` of `spec`, and `spec` generates the code from them itself: `api` takes it as the nested Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec/contracts` at the tag `contracts/vX.Y.Z` with the number of the same tag of `spec`, and does not generate its own. The submodule, the module `spec` and the contracts module are moved together, by one move to a tag. With `app` `api` talks by Connect, with unary calls and binary protobuf on the wire, and the contract `app` ↔ `api` is the package `novatrail.api.v1` of `spec`. Modules and packages `api` reads through `module-kit`, at the version `go.mod` pins, and the types of `spec` of the same tag, and the code of `api` downloads and lays them out, the command `api packages`. Module packages come from the releases of `modules` by the digests from the index of the release that the configuration of `api` pins. A tag of `tutor` in which a new model is admitted `api` takes only after the merge of the pull request of `modules` that ran the eval sets of all the modules on that tag. A new version of `learner-model` that breaks the API of `engine` comes together with a version of `engine`, and `api` moves to both together. `infra` rolls out `api`, by a list of exact versions and digests, and `api` is rolled back only by rolling forward. A release of `api` puts out signed static binaries for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64` and the `Dockerfile`, and the deployment of `infra` builds the image from them — on `gcr.io/distroless/static-debian13:nonroot` by digest, without a shell and not as root; in the image there are only the binary and the module packages. The operator's jobs run with the same image, so the migrations and the operator's commands are commands of the `api` binary itself;
- CI holds the shared checks of `.github` — secret scanning, DCO, CodeQL, Scorecard and the documents check `docs-check-repo` —, the Go checks of its shared `go.yml` with coverage in Codecov, the jobs of its own and SonarCloud. All but Scorecard hold the merge, the findings of CodeQL hold it by a rule of the ruleset, and the merge is only by a merge commit. Code comes with its tests, and the merge waits for `codecov/patch`. The badges of these checks and of the release are the README's;
- stage 2 is "The first slice with a learner", together with `tutor`, `canvas`, `app`, `skill-map` and `infra`. The first app is the build of `app` with `api` on which the author solves tasks in a browser themselves: with handwriting, lessons and the engine, with sign-in, locally in the devcontainer, without children and without a deployment, and the database of `api` lies outside the repository. While the admitted models stand at the rung "author" of the admission rule of `tutor`, the AI reads only the author's work: before a model reads the work of anyone else — by platform AI or with an own key —, it passes the rung "full" on the samples of adult volunteers (П-16). The order of the tasks of all the repositories is the cross-repository plan, `COORDINATION.md` of `spec`. Before the first child come verifiable parental consent and the agreements with AI providers, П-1 and П-3: a signed form, whose scan the operator checks, and before the public launch also a card transaction, with the consent methods of the contract, — and the DPIA. The data region is the USA, one place for all learners: Cloud Run `us-east4`, Neon `aws-us-east-1`. The DPIA is needed also before the first learner from the EU or the United Kingdom, if one comes earlier, and until it the slice accepts no learners from there.

## 2. To decide when detailed

Every item of the card is decided: its thirteen items by the ten subsections below, each by a task of epic E1, whose bodies are in [plan/E1.md](plan/E1.md). A subsection states the question and how it was decided; the design, with the facts it proceeds from, follows them. The subsections keep their numbers, which the archives cite; 2.2, 2.7 and 2.10 join two items of the card each, which cannot be decided apart. A question that goes beyond this repository goes «в реестр spec», a row of [run/QUESTIONS.md](../run/QUESTIONS.md).

The card's one item left to a later stage: the predicates of the checks of third-party modules in a separate process with a memory limit — stage 4 (О-9). Put off on purpose, each a row of [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md) with when to return: sign-in through Apple, by the answer of `spec` to Т-41 (Д3), a general OIDC adapter (Д4), and tariffs beyond "closed slice" and payment, stage 3 (Д10; О-14). The real-time channel returns with voice, at stage 3 (Т-25). Of the questions of the register of `spec` the items rested on, О-14 and Т-25 are open.

### 2.1. Sign-in and permissions

- **Gist:** which sign-in service, how `api` checks its token and how the permissions are built — who may do what, and for which learner —, so that at stage 5 the permissions of teachers lie on the same model.
- **Decided:** the sign-in service and the token check; the permissions, the actions of an adult and of a learner, the checks before a learner's data and access to the closed slice, which close П-17.

### 2.2. Database and learner model: where it lives (П-7) and what is stored about it (Т-22)

- **Gist:** which database `api` has; where the learner model lives — in the platform's database, in a family's file or on the device (П-7); what `api` stores about it — the state of the skills or an append-only evidence log by which the state is recomputed (Т-22). These are two items of the card, but they cannot be decided apart: Т-22 is tied to П-7, and the log and the state lie where П-7 decides. `api` chooses the database itself.
- **Decided:** the database, the OpenFGA store beside it, the database of the first app and the retention periods; where the learner model lives and what is stored about it, which close П-7 and Т-22. The design below holds the table "what lies — where — period — into the export".

### 2.3. Which version of a module a new attempt gets

- **Gist:** the last published version of the module or the one pinned to the learner's connection of the module. In either case the attempt stores the digest of its package. On the answer depends when the learner gets a fix of the module and when a major version with a mapping table reaches them. At stage 2 the image of `api` holds the packages.
- **Decided:** which version a new attempt and a lesson's task get, which versions the image holds, the configuration `modules.json` and the attempts on a withdrawn version and outside the window.

### 2.4. Carrying progress over after a major version of a module

- **Gist:** a major version of a module carries a mapping table from old skill IDs to new ones, so that the learner's progress does not break: when `api` transfers progress, over what — the evidence log or the state of the skills —, and what becomes of a skill to which the table gives no counterpart, `replaced_by: []`. How the transfer is computed is `learner-model`'s; when it happens and over which data is `api`'s.
- **Decided:** the transfer is part of every computation of the state by the log, by `retired_skills` of the current version's graph, and the log does not change.

### 2.5. Showing the solution (О-7) and a hint at the learner's request (О-21)

- **Gist:** whether `api` shows the task's solution at the learner's request and under what conditions (О-7), what the request "Hint" gives after a wrong answer and what "explain differently" gives (О-21), and which answer slot the fallback text speaks about when a submission has several wrong ones. The questions are of the register of `spec`; the hint ladder, the offer to show the solution and everything that goes to the device are `api`'s. The pause is not `api`'s: `spec` decides it.
- **Decided:** showing the solution; the hint on request, "explain differently", the answer slot of the fallback text and the end of the attempt, which close О-7 and О-21.

### 2.6. Tariffs (О-14); payment — stage 3

- **Gist:** in the first slice there are no tariffs and no payment. The recommendation of О-14 is platform AI without payment before the beta, within a quota, for the participants of the closed slice. So here the quota of stage 2 is decided — whose it is, what it counts, when it resets and what the learner sees when it has run out —, so that the tariffs and payment of stage 3 lie on it without rework. The tariffs themselves are О-14, a question of the register of `spec`; payment is a part of `api` and an external provider, stage 3.
- **Decided:** the quota rule — the unit, whose it is, the caps, the family day, the tariff and admission, the exhausted quota, a task midway and AI that is closed —; the reserve, the settlement, the counters, the periods, the export, deletion and logs.

### 2.7. The protocol between `app` and `api` and the real-time channel (Т-25)

- **Gist:** which requests `app` sends to `api` and what comes in reply, and whether a stream of events from `api` to `app` is needed. These are two items of the card, but they cannot be decided apart: without a channel the verdict and the tutor's words are two requests, and with one the protocol would be different. The contract itself is `spec`'s package `novatrail.api.v1`, protobuf and buf; the channel is `spec`'s open question Т-25.
- **Decided:** the requests of the first slice, the boundary with the contract of `spec`, refusals and outcomes, idempotency keys and what goes to the device; binary protobuf on the wire, the open views through Connect GET, the files of the device part, the withdrawal of a version, CORS and logs; no real-time channel in the first slice, the check of handwriting by two requests. The design below holds the table of requests.

### 2.8. Deletion by a fresh sign-in

- **Gist:** deletion is confirmed by a fresh sign-in at the sign-in service, not by the PIN of the section for adults. To decide: what counts as a fresh sign-in, what is deleted and in what order — the profile of one learner or the whole account —, what remains and for what period, how the other devices learn of it and how deletion survives a failure midway.
- **Decided:** a fresh sign-in, the refusal class of the token check, the order of the checks and the codes "fresh sign-in needed" and "account gone"; deleting a profile and an account, the operator's job that finishes what did not go through, deletion by period, the minimal record of a child consent, users of the sign-in service without an account and the export. The design below holds the table "where what remains — how long".

### 2.9. Lesson

- **Gist:** how the app gets the segments and tasks of a lesson and what `api` records about a passed lesson. In the first app tasks come in lessons.
- **Decided:** `api` leads a lesson by a lesson run, one per profile and lesson, on the package on which the lesson began, and `app` holds the place among the explanations and examples.

### 2.10. The path of a task with a rubric: feedback that did not pass the rule, and an essay that cannot be read (П-19)

- **Gist:** what the learner sees in place of rubric feedback that did not pass the feedback rule, and how they see an essay that cannot be read and what is recorded about it. These are two items of the card, but they cannot be decided apart: both are about the path of a task with a rubric, which has no verdict, and both come down to what the server stores of such an attempt, П-19.
- **Decided:** the path, how a submission with a rubric ends, the phrase "no feedback this time", rewriting and the learner's "Done"; what the server stores of such an attempt and who reads the criteria's marks, which close П-19.

## 3. Design

How the component is built by the items above. The first ten subsections are the decisions on them, each with the facts it proceeds from: sign-in and permissions, the database and the learner model, the versions of modules and the packages in the image, carrying progress over after a major version of a module, showing the solution and hints, the platform AI quota, the lesson, the path of a task with a rubric, the protocol between `app` and `api`, deletion and the export. The last is the service as a whole: its parts and what each needs from outside.

### 3.1. Sign-in and permissions

The facts about the services are in the records sources 2.1, 2.2 and 10.2, and the pages for the code in 2.4 and 2.6; the precedents are in [canvas-api at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs).

**The sign-in service:**
- Firebase Auth. The dev project of `api` is without the upgrade to Identity Platform, and the platform's production project works with Identity Platform: `infra` upgrades and configures it by code — the authorized domains `<project>.firebaseapp.com` and `<project>.web.app`, sign-in only by the Google window, email, phone and anonymous sign-in off, the activity log off, and only `api` deletes a user. The service account of `api` gets the role `signInUsers` — `firebaseauth.users.get`, `.update` and `.delete` — exactly for the four calls of Identity Toolkit that the Admin SDK of `api` makes: `accounts:lookup` — the revocation check of each ID token, `accounts:batchGet` — iterating over users in `api sweep`, `accounts:update` — revoking tokens, `accounts:delete` — deleting a user (the source files `auth/auth.go`, `auth/user_mgt.go` and `auth/export_users.go` of SDK v4.22.0, read 2026-11-09). [build.md](build.md) names them for `infra`, which grants the role, and a new call comes as a row «в реестр spec» before the release that makes it. Sign-in is through Google alone; Apple waits for Т-41 (Д3). There is no email and password, no email links and no phone.
- `api` calls the sign-in service through a narrow port of the Admin SDK for Go, `firebase.google.com/go/v4`. The port does four things: it checks the token and gives the UID of the sign-in service, `auth_time` and the refusal class, revokes tokens, deletes a user, and iterates over users with the time of the last sign-in and of the token refresh — for the operator's job. The subject of the permissions is the account ID in `api`, and the UID of the sign-in service lies only in the account's record: another sign-in service changes the adapter, not the permissions and the data. There is no shared OIDC adapter for self-hosting in the first slice (Д4).
- The port is the package `internal/authn`, the adapter is the Admin SDK for Go, at the version of `go.mod`. Revoking tokens and deleting a user are one operation of the port, `RemoveUser`. The adapter's tests run on the real SDK against a fake Google, without an emulator, and the adapter makes the binary about one and a half times larger (measurements 1.2). The service account key is the secret `API_FIREBASE_CREDENTIALS`, only of the type `service_account` and only outside a deployment, and in Cloud Run the service's credentials are used, without a key ([`auth/auth.go`](https://github.com/firebase/firebase-admin-go/blob/v4.22.0/auth/auth.go) of the Admin SDK v4.22.0; [`option/option.go`](https://github.com/googleapis/google-api-go-client/blob/v0.279.0/option/option.go) of google-api-go-client v0.279.0).
- The app reads the parameters of the sign-in service from the deployment configuration — this is an item of the card of `app`.

**The token check:**

| Requests | Check | Why |
|---|---|---|
| the files of the device part of a package — media files, `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` — and the open views of a package by digest: the manifest, the skill graph, the technique catalog, the solving steps | without a token | this is the content of modules, not a learner's data, and the official modules lie openly in the releases of `modules` anyway. The element `canvas` gets a picture from `app` as a `Blob` — by the property `media`, a function that `app` gives from the files of the device part —, and `api` answers CORS on all paths, without a token too. A request with `Authorization` goes with a preflight, and on the own origin of `api` each address would get a preflight of its own; without a token and headers, the `fetch` of a file, which goes through the HTTP cache, and the Connect GET of an open view go without one. A withdrawn version `api` does not serve: a file gets `410`, an open view the refusal "version withdrawn". With modules of third-party authors — revisit (stage 4; О-10) |
| all the others, the lesson and the task among them | `VerifyIDTokenAndCheckRevoked`: the signature by Google's keys from the cache, and the revocation, by a network call | after revocation an ID token lives up to an hour more, and the token of a deleted account must neither create it anew, nor read data, nor spend the quota |
| irreversible ones: deleting a profile and an account, changing a forgotten PIN | with revocation, and a fresh sign-in: `auth_time` no older than 5 minutes | a PIN is not a confirmation. Otherwise the refusal "fresh sign-in needed" |

Before deleting the user at the sign-in service comes `RevokeRefreshTokens`, then `DeleteUser`, as steps of the deletion of an account. The token check gives the UID, `auth_time` and the refusal class — "expired", "invalid", "revoked", "no user", "disabled" or "failure" —, and by the class `api` answers "account gone", `unauthenticated` without a cause, or `unavailable`.

**The permissions model** — OpenFGA:

```text
model
  schema 1.1

type user

type classroom
  relations
    define teacher: [user]

type learner
  relations
    define owner: [user]
    define classroom: [classroom]
    define can_manage: owner
    define can_learn: owner
    define can_view: owner or teacher from classroom
```

- `user` is an account of `api` by its ID; `learner` is a learner's profile. `api` writes the tuple `owner` when it creates each profile of the account, the profile of the owner themselves too.
- `can_learn` — learning requests, `can_manage` — an adult's actions on a profile, `can_view` — reading progress: at stage 5 also by the teacher of a class.
- `classroom` and `teacher` are for stage 5: the model describes them in advance, and the first slice writes no tuples for them.
- Attempts, lessons and everything else about a profile are rows of the database with the profile's ID, not objects of OpenFGA: `api` checks the permission on the profile and reads the row with this ID.
- OpenFGA works as a library in the process of `api`, not as a sidecar. The store is Postgres, a separate database `openfga` in the same Neon project as the database of `api`. The store's migrations are OpenFGA's own files, `assets`, which the command `api migrate` runs by the same goose with a session lock as the migrations of the database of `api`; `RunMigrations` does not fit: it is without a lock, and its package brings into the binary a MySQL driver under MPL-2.0 (sources 2.4; [`migrate.go`](https://github.com/openfga/openfga/blob/v1.22.1/pkg/storage/migrate/migrate.go) of OpenFGA v1.22.1). OpenFGA makes the binary almost three times larger (measurements 1.1). The store of `api` and the model are also created by `migrate`, under its lock, and `serve` at start only finds the store and, among its models, the built-in one: if there is none, it does not start. The model, like the schema, changes by the rule "first add, then remove". The model is the file `internal/authz/model.fga`, this block verbatim, and a test holds one to the other. The version of OpenFGA is exact, in `go.mod`.
- The tuple is written in a transaction of the database, before `COMMIT`: OpenFGA has its own database, so the tuple is written from inside the open transaction of the database of `api`, after the profile's rows. If it was not written, the transaction rolls back; if it was written and `COMMIT` did not go through, a "dangling" tuple remains of a profile that is not in the database. It gives permissions to nothing, and the operator's daily job removes it.

**Actions of an adult and of a learner.** On a shared device there is one token — the account owner's —, and the server does not know who holds the device. So kinds of actions are distinguished:

| Action | What the request carries | Permission |

| showing the solution to a child while the setting "ask before showing" is on | the profile's ID | `can_manage`; a learning request to show `api` rejects. If the setting is off or the learner is of the category `adult`, it is learning, `can_learn` |

| which digests the image no longer gives | — | the account from the token |

An adult confirms showing the solution to a child on this model: `app` asks for the PIN and sends the showing as an adult action, and the request carries no proof of the PIN.

**PIN and П-17:**
- The PIN is a barrier of the interface, not a security boundary. `app` keeps the section for adults behind it, and `app` decides when to ask for the PIN.
- The PIN is 4–6 digits, its hash is argon2id, failed attempts are no more than 5 in 15 minutes. `api` keeps the hash, the counter and the lock time in the account: so the PIN is one for all devices, and changing the device does not reset the lock. A forgotten PIN is changed by a fresh sign-in. The hash is argon2id at the OWASP minimum, as a PHC string; failures are counted over the last 15 minutes, and the fifth closes input for 15 minutes from itself; the PIN requests of one account go in turn, under `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` of the account's row.
- The owner profile on a shared device `app` opens behind the same PIN as the section for adults. `api` accepts an own key only with a request of a profile of the category `adult` (Т-9; О-4), and the server checks this rule itself. Whether a device is shared or not, the server does not know. П-17 has two sides: this rule of the server and the rule of `app` on the device. The owner profile on an account that has a profile of another category `app` opens with the same PIN at each opening, and the own key lies on the device only if the key's owner decided so on this device, otherwise in the tab's memory.

**Checks before a learner's data**, in order. On each request with a token, first comes the refusal class of the token check, and then the mark "being deleted": for a marked account any request, both creating an account and redeeming a code, gets "account gone". Then:
1. The token — by the table above. A UID without an account can only sign in: redeem an invite code and create an account.
2. Access to the closed slice: the account has redeemed a one-time invite code. The operator creates the codes by a command of `api`, the database holds only the SHA-256 of the code, at the first sign-in the code is bound to the account, and `api` does not read the email. The code is 16 characters of Crockford's base32, 80 bits, and the command is `api invite`: it is run from one's own terminal, not as a job, because the output of a Cloud Run job is kept in its log, and with it the code. Before admission `api` creates neither profiles nor learners' data. A code is created with a tariff — by default "closed slice" —, and at redemption the tariff passes to the account, and the tariff sets the caps of the quota.
3. The account owner is an adult: an account is created with the owner's statement that they are 18 or older, and `api` stores the date of the statement. For a child's profile, adulthood is confirmed by the consent method itself — a signed form, and before the public launch also a card transaction.

A request with a profile ID then goes on like this:

4. The right in OpenFGA — `can_learn`, `can_manage` or `can_view` on the profile. It is checked before anything that `api` reads by this ID, and another's profile and a profile that does not exist get one and the same refusal: from the answer one cannot learn whether the profile exists and what status its consent has.
5. The profile's consent is given: without it, no learning requests.
6. The AI route — by the profile's age category: admission is computed by the admission function of `tutor`. `api` calls it before an attempt, and `tutor` before each call of a model with the learner's work; the rules are the admission table of `spec`, `admission/`, and the deployment facts are the configuration file of `api`, which `api` reads at start: a fact holds at a provider for the named categories, without the file none holds, and the cells of platform AI that are open given facts are closed, while an adult's own key is open; a fact that the table's vocabulary does not know is an error at start (the type of the file and how to read it are `tutor`'s, by the text of the [admission table](../spec/admission/README.md)). The lowest rung of the models' admission rule, which `Table.Select` takes, is also named by the deployment: "author" in the first app and on the deployment `novatrail`, and otherwise "full". The facts file and the rung are held by `infra`, beside the rollout list, and the deployment passes them to the revision, and `api` reads them at start; they are not part of the image or of the git of `api`. No value of the rung means "full", closed by default, and what is missing `api` writes at start to the log, for the operator. In code: the file is an absolute path in `API_ADMISSION_FACTS_FILE`, outside the git working tree, the rung is `API_ADMISSION_RUNG`, `author` or `full`; `author` is taken on a deployment too, and only the stub of `tutor` is refused there. The mode of reading handwriting is `API_READING_MODE`, `pair` or `single`, `pair` when unset, which `tutor` takes as `Config.Reading`: either holds at either rung, and at `full`, where the table admits no model of the pair, a reading is "not admitted", not a refusal of the configuration. What is missing is said by the line `startup` — by the fields `facts_file` and `rung_set` —, and the command `api check` checks the file and the rung before migrations, without serving requests. The admission function does not get the country: a country where the law requires more than a statement of age `api` closes before an attempt, like a parent's consent. An own key with a request of a profile whose category is not `adult` `api` does not accept (Т-9; О-4). The fact `minor_safeguards` is stated by the deployment only when the provider's list of measures is met and the author has decided by analysis that they are enough, by the text of the [admission table](../spec/admission/README.md).
7. The platform AI quota — on the requests that call platform AI, and on the start of a task with AI, which needs a reserve for a typical task. An exhausted quota does not refuse a submission or words: it changes what the learner gets — an outcome in a successful answer. The refusal "quota" is got only by the start of an attempt and of a segment and by "explain differently" without a reserve.

Irreversible actions check fresh sign-in last, after the right: then a repeat of a deletion after a lost answer gets "account gone" or "no profile" without a new sign-in window.

**Where this is in code** ([connect-go v2.0.2](https://github.com/connectrpc/connect-go/blob/v2.0.2/docs/v2-guide.md), [the contracts at `contracts/v0.12.0`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/blob/contracts/v0.12.0/contracts/novatrail/api/v1/apiv1connect/account.connect.go)). The token class, the mark, the account (step 1), the right (step 4) and fresh sign-in are checked by a Connect interceptor before the handler, by the table of methods `internal/rpc/methods.go`: which method needs a token, whether it needs an account — without one, only creating an account —, which right on the profile of its message it needs, and whether it needs a fresh sign-in. The right is on the message, before any reading by profile ID, and the profile's row under the account the interceptor reads only after the right: a dangling tuple gives the same refusal "no profile". Steps 2, 3, 5–7 belong to the handlers of their own tasks. Access and the statement (steps 2 and 3) are `CreateAccount` of the package `internal/account`: the code is read by `internal/invite`, and the redemption, the binding and the tariff are done by one transaction, `store.CreateAccount`. Creating profiles, child consent and settings are `internal/account`: the country is checked by `store.CountryAccepted`, and the tuple `owner` is written from inside the transaction, before `COMMIT`. Consent, the AI route and the quota (steps 5–7) are checked by `internal/learning`, with `internal/admission` and `internal/quota`.

Creating a profile is an action on the account, and it goes like this:

- **The country before the DPIA.** The learner's country is given by the account owner, and `api` checks it against the list of countries that an operator command keeps — when a profile is created and when the country changes. Before the DPIA the slice does not accept learners from the EU and the UK. The list is the allowed countries in the database of `api`, empty at start: a country where the law requires more than a statement of age is closed until the operator opens it. The command `api countries` does not open before the DPIA the EU with its regions, which have codes of their own, nor the EEA, nor the UK — 42 codes.
- **Consent — for each learner**, by the table of the privacy document of `spec` of who gives consent at which age. Where a parent's consent is needed, `api` first records a consent in the status "waiting" under the account — with the age category and the country, without a pseudonym and the other fields of the profile. While there is no profile, this consent is seen, edited and withdrawn by the account from the token. The profile with the tuple `owner` `api` creates only when the operator has confirmed the consent. Verifiable parental consent is a signed form, whose scan the operator checks and keeps themselves, outside the repositories and outside the database of `api`, and before the public launch a card transaction is added to it; the form does not ask for the child's name, only for the ID of the consent record. An account has no more than 10 consents "waiting": a request over the limit gets `resource_exhausted` with the cause `CAUSE_TOO_MANY_CONSENTS`, and a repeat with the same key gets the same consent, at the limit too. An adult learner gives consent themselves when they create their own profile: a consent row confirmed at once, the method being the owner's statement with the account's date. A child's category before the DPIA is by the boundary of 13 years: 0–12 — `under_13`, 13–17 — `from_consent_to_18`, and `from_13_to_consent` is empty while the EU and the UK are closed. Above the age of digital consent the learner gives consent themselves, in their own profile, not the account owner; how a consent is given, withdrawn and cancelled is the consent contract in code below. Before the first child the DPIA and agreements with AI providers are needed too, so the operator does not confirm children's consents yet.

### 3.2. Database and learner model

The facts about Neon and about the precedents are in the records sources 3.1, 3.2 and 3.5, and in [canvas-api at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs).

**Database:**
- Neon Postgres, as in canvas-api. There is one project, and it has two databases: `api` — the data of `api` — and `openfga` — the OpenFGA store. They share the region and the history window.
- Neon scales to zero, as `spec` requires of the platform's database: compute goes to sleep after 5 minutes idle and wakes up on the next request, usually within a few hundred milliseconds. On the Launch plan this can be turned off.
- The region is the USA: Neon `aws-us-east-1`, Cloud Run `us-east4`. The Neon dev project of `api` is also in `aws-us-east-1`. Neon works only in AWS: it no longer creates projects in Azure, and Google Cloud is not on its list (sources 3.1). So the database's region is a Neon region near the Cloud Run region, like the pair `aws-us-east-1` and `us-east4` of canvas-api.
- Connections are only over TLS: Neon accepts no others. The requests of `serve` go through Neon's pooler — PgBouncer in transaction mode, without `SET`, `LISTEN`, `PREPARE` at the SQL level and session locks, but with the protocol's prepared statements, which is what pgx uses, — and migrations go directly: goose holds a session lock. So there are two connection strings per database: for `api`, `API_DATABASE_URL` of the pooler and the direct `API_MIGRATE_DATABASE_URL`, for `openfga`, `API_OPENFGA_DATABASE_URL` and `API_MIGRATE_OPENFGA_DATABASE_URL`, all of them secrets and all with `sslmode=verify-full`: in pgx `require` does not check the certificate.
- The migrations of both databases are the operator's migrations job. The tool is goose v3, the driver pgx v5, and the migrations are the command `api migrate`, embedded in the binary and only forward: the schema changes by the rule "add first, then remove", and only a migration with the mark `-- contract:` removes ([README](https://github.com/pressly/goose/blob/v3.29.0/README.md) of goose v3.29.0). A deployment runs it before the revision takes requests ([build.md](build.md)).
- Backups are Neon's history window: within it the database can be restored to any moment, and what was deleted lives in the history until it leaves the window. On Free the window is 6 hours, on Launch a day by default, and at most 7 days. `api` makes no snapshots of the database: a snapshot would keep what was deleted after the window too. A snapshot with a term of its own comes only by a change of this decision on the database. The deployment is on the Launch plan with a window of 1 day: what was deleted leaves the history within a day.
- The first app — a Neon dev project on the Free plan, which the author creates. The connection strings lie outside the repository, not in the folder `/workspaces/api`: `api` takes them from a variable or from a file whose path is named by a variable with the suffix `_FILE`.
- Tests of code with the database — `embedded-postgres` v2: a real Postgres 17 without Docker, the same in the container and in CI, and the Neon dev project runs the same major version, — one server per test package and a database of its own for each test, a copy of a template database through which the migrations went once ([README](https://github.com/fergusstrange/embedded-postgres/blob/v2.0.1/README.md) of embedded-postgres v2.0.1). The library checks Postgres against the sums of its manifest, and `go.sum` holds the manifest. The supervisor is built by the tests from the module's source files and passed through `Config.Supervisor`: a downloaded one the library would check against `checksums.txt` of the same release at run time.

**Learner model:**
- It lives in the database of `api`, is kept as an append-only evidence log, and the skills' state is a cache (П-7, Т-22).
- **Evidence** — one per finished attempt: the outcome of the attempt, not of a single submission. An attempt of a task with a rubric gives no evidence in the first slice: feedback by a rubric has no verdict, and by the recommendation of О-17 it does not go into the model. Nor does an attempt closed "left", "abandoned" or "not suitable" give evidence, if it had no wrong answers. What is in it and how much it weighs is decided by `learner-model`, and `api` stores its type as it is. Beside it are columns for `api` itself: the profile ID, the attempt ID, the package digest, the module, the time and the sequence number in the profile's log. There is no content in evidence — neither what was read nor the tutor's words. An attempt of a task from the learner's paper gives none: its task is of no module and has no skill.
- **The adult's correction** of a module's level is a fact of the log beside the attempts: the module, the band set, and when, at every profile of the account, which every learner sees on the skill map. It gives no evidence of a skill, and `learner-model` sets the level in that band in one step; it goes into the export and is deleted with the log.
- **Append-only.** `api` neither changes nor deletes the log's rows one by one:
  - the evidence of attempts on a version that the pinned index marked `wrong_key` is cut off by a filter at recomputation, and the rows remain. The filter reads the mark on each entry of the index, on the entries outside the format window of `api` too;
  - the carry-over by `retired_skills` after a major version also happens at recomputation and at appending, and the log does not change;
  - the log is deleted only whole — with the profile or by its term.
- **The state cache** — a row per profile: the state, the version of `learner-model`, the release of `modules` pinned in the configuration, the format version window of `api` and the number of the last evidence taken into account. If the version, the release or the window do not match those on which `api` runs now, the state is recomputed from the log: the current versions of modules depend on the release and the window, and with them the carry-over by `retired_skills`. `learner-model` gets the graphs of the current versions of all the modules of the profile's log — connected or not, suitable for the learner or not, — so the set of graphs is set by the log, the release and the window, not by the profile's settings. New evidence is appended to the cache without recomputing the whole log: this requires of `learner-model` that the appended state match the recomputed one — a condition `spec` sets, and otherwise Т-22 comes back. So it is too with the evidence of an attempt begun on the previous version of a module and ended after the transition: it names the skills of the previous version, and what is appended is carried over the same way as at recomputation. Evidence and the cache are written by one transaction, and the profile's cache row is locked in it: the evidence's sequence number is the next after the last in the profile's log. So two attempts of one profile that ended at the same instant on two devices do not get one number and do not lose what was appended. The lock is taken by the previous image during a deployment too, though it does not overwrite a cache with a stamp newer than its own. The cache row is created together with the profile, empty, with the number 0, so that the first evidence too has something to lock. If the stamp matches, and the log has evidence after the last one taken into account, `api` first appends this tail to the cache in order, with carry-over, — both on reading and before appending new evidence: otherwise the new evidence would jump over the tail. Recomputation is lazy: a profile is recomputed at the first reading after a change of version, release or window, and a stamp newer than its own — by release or window — the image does not overwrite: while a deployment is going on, the previous image computes the state for the answer without writing it. A recomputation costs one learner's log. A key more precise than the release — the `wrong_key` marks and the `retired_skills` tables that concern the profile — comes only if a measurement shows too many recomputations. A snapshot of the state, so as not to recompute the log from the start, is `learner-model`'s to keep or not; if there is one, it is valid only under the same stamp, and carry-over is not applied to it.
- **The engine choice** stores the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the package digests — of all the graphs that `learner-model` and the engine got, — the seed field and the reason code from the closed list `ReasonCode` and the grade by which the model computed the starting point of modules without evidence, and beyond that the release of `modules` and the number of the last evidence by which the choice was made. Without them a repeat from the log does not know which prefix of the log to take and which withdrawal marks were in force then. The choice also stores its time: by it a repeat restores the lesson runs that the engine saw — their start, end reason and end time do not change, and which segments had ended by then is said by the close times of the attempts. The close reason and the close time of an attempt, once recorded, do not change, so the closed attempts of tasks with a rubric that the engine gets as input a repeat restores by the same time. The choice also stores whether the pool was limited to tasks checked without AI: otherwise a repeat does not know from which pool the engine chose. The release of `modules`, the number of the last evidence, the pool and the time of the choice are named by the platform's architecture in `spec`, and so is the format window in the cache mark. The choice also stores these fields, one of them empty:
  - the skill chosen on the skill map: a choice from the map holds until the learner asks for something else, the record carries it into the next one, and `api` calls the engine with it both for `next` and at the end of a lesson run, whatever code the choice came out with;
  - the profile's age category at the time of the choice: it changes at the yearly restatement and at a change of country, and `api` selects the modules, the lessons and the task pool by it before the engine;
  - a flag of which of the digests are engine modules, connected and suitable for the learner: the digests also name the modules of the log, whose graphs only `learner-model` gets;
  - the seed field is empty while the engine takes no randomness, and it takes none: that this is so is seen from the version of `engine` in the same record.

  This adds no learner data: `api` already has the category and the connections.
- **The summary of issues** — an input of the engine beyond the learner model, which `api` assembles from its records of choices and attempts rather than stores:
  - for a skill — the time of its last issue, and the issue of a lesson is an issue of each of its skills; if the skill's last closed attempt is a probe or a repetition, its result as facts: how many items where the skill is primary got a first verdict, how many of them "correct", and whether the solution was shown. "Failure" by them is computed by the engine, not by `api`;
  - for a module — the number of choices: a task and a lesson count one each;
  - for a task — when its solution last went to the device: such a task the engine does not give "right again", that is, does not give as the next task.

  A repeat of a choice assembles the summary anew from the records up to the time of the choice — they do not change. Of the attempts with a rubric the engine gets only those closed "Done".
- **The export** carries the log, the current state and the choices with reason codes; the export format is `spec`'s, `export/`.

A submission with a verdict is a row of `submissions`: its answer — the verdicts, what was read and the solved task, — and the record of the submission's key refers to it rather than holds the answer.

In code the log and the cache are kept by the package `internal/model` over `learner-model` at the version of `go.mod`: a module's level forgets after a pause of 14 days, and three rules of `spec` hold — a module's level starts a band below the band of the learner's grade, an adult's correction sets it in a band in one step, and three failures in a row past the trial series lower it by a band. Evidence is written by the catch-up of owed evidence: under `FOR UPDATE` of the cache row — which it creates if there is none, — it appends the evidence of all the closed attempts of the profile that owe it and whose package the image holds, in the order of closing, with the number after the last in the log. It is called by a closing in a request, in the same transaction, by the skill map and progress before reading, and by the step `evidence` of the job `api sweep`, so evidence is got also by the attempts that a job, a trigger or the previous image during a deployment closed. The cache mark is the version of `learner-model` from the build, the release of `modules` and the format window: if it matches, the tail of the log is appended; if older, recomputation from the log; if newer, the state for the answer without writing. The evidence of a version withdrawn as `wrong_key` is in the log, not in the cache. An adult's correction of a module's level is a row of the log of no attempt, migration `0021`: `LevelCorrection` of `learner-model`, of the package of the module's current version, under an ID of its own, `correction_id`, which the export names it by in place of an attempt's; it is folded whatever its package, a wrong key too, and goes with the log. An attempt whose package has left the image gets no evidence (Д27).

In code the record of a choice is a row of `engine_choices` with the fields of migrations `0010` and `0011`: the reason code, the skills that the choice issued, the package digest on which it was made, the skill from the map, the age category and the grade, whether the pool was limited to tasks checked by tapping, the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the release of `modules`, the format window, the number of the last evidence and the digests of the graphs, and among them those of the engine modules. There is no seed field: the engine takes no randomness. The references `after_attempt_id` and `after_lesson_run_id` name the closing and the end after which the choice was given as the next one — by them a repeat gives the same choice. "Nothing to give" is a row of its own kind, `nothing`, with the outcome mark "not admitted" and a cause: "not admitted", "quota" with the time of the next day, or "platform limit" (migrations `0011` and `0016`); in the answer it has no ID. The summary of issues is assembled from the records at each choice: the time of a skill's issue — by the skills that the record stores.

**Retention periods** (П-9):
- Attempts, observations, lesson runs, the log and the choices live while the profile lives, and a profile without activity for 12 months is deleted with everything about it. An open run without actions for 30 days ends "abandoned": it is closed by the check on request and by the operator's daily retention job, and the row remains. Activity is a learning request with the profile ID or an adult action on the profile; `api` stores its date, without the time.
- An account not signed in to for 12 months is deleted with its profiles and the user at the sign-in service, and of the child consents minimal records remain, without the account and profile IDs — for 5 years, and then the job erases them. "Not signed in" `api` reads as "there were no requests with the account's token": the sign-in time in the token, `auth_time`, changes only at a new sign-in, and the sign-in service refreshes the token itself. Otherwise the account of an owner who learns every day but has not signed in anew for a year would be deleted. Without a request one cannot learn, so the profiles of such an account are by then without activity too.
- An essay has no read text, no justifications and no feedback words at all: they live in the request's memory, and on the server the criteria's marks and the source of the words remain.
- The words of hints live until the end of the attempt and are deleted when it closes, and the rung number and the source remain. An attempt ends also when the profile has begun another, and an abandoned one — 7 days without actions — is closed by the check on request and by the operator's daily retention job. A closing by the job does not move the profile's activity date. An adult sees progress and the repeating traps, not the tutor's words.
- A quota counter is needed for its family day and one more day after its end — to account for requests across midnight, — and then the retention job deletes it: a counter lives no longer than 2 days after the end of its day, and leaves Neon's history a day later.
- Deletion is done by the operator's daily retention job, once a day, by the same path as deletion on request, only without fresh sign-in, and it checks the term under the row's lock. It also finishes the deletions of accounts whose steps after the transaction did not go through, and deletes the users of the sign-in service without an account of `api` who have not signed in or refreshed the token for 30 days, and those created and never signed in, 30 days after creation. `api` sends no emails, so the term is named by the consent text, as in canvas-api. What was deleted leaves Neon's history by the window, and an honest deletion term names it too.

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- Everything that carries a profile ID is pseudonymous personal data, not anonymous. The content of work is only the observations, and until the end of the attempt also the words of hints; an essay has no observation either — only the criteria's marks. There is no crop in the database: it lives in the request's memory. A stored work, its picture with it, is there only while its storage is on: `api` checks its form alone, reads nothing more of it, writes none of it to the logs and gives it to no AI provider, as the consent text for the storage of works promises.
- The learner model is in the database, on the server. The row "Learner model" of the privacy document's table of data says "the server". The model does not go to AI, and neither IDs nor content are written to the logs of `api`.
- The database's processor is Neon, part of Databricks: its processor agreement is Neon's schedule to the Databricks DPA, and AWS is a subprocessor of Databricks (sources 3.1). The schedule says nothing of children's data. The platform has no lawyer: by Claude's reading this agreement is the written assurance that COPPA §312.8(c) expects from a service provider (sources 3.4 spec), — so it is recorded in the row "Hosting and infrastructure" of the privacy document's list of processors.

**In code** — migrations `0012` and `0013`: `consents` gets who gave it, the request code, the form's state, `applied` and the text version, and the check "a profile only for a confirmed one" is removed; the new tables are `consent_forms` (where the scan lies and until what day), `account_texts`, `work_storage_consents` and `problem_reports`, whose references to the account, the profile and the attempt become empty at deletion. The daily job erases the scans after their term and the problem reports a year after the review.

**In code** — migration `0017`: the table `works`, keyed by the profile and the attempt, with references to both that delete with them. `PutWork` holds the profile's row `FOR SHARE` and keeps a work while `work_storage` is true; switching off updates that row before it erases the works, so either waits for the other: a work put before the switch is erased by it, and one after is refused.

### 3.3. Module versions and packages in the image

The facts about the CI checks and the scheduled job the design rests on are in the records sources 4.2 and 4.4.

**What the decision starts from:**
- an attempt stores the digest of its package — the digest of its inventory, — the module format version, the version of `module-kit` and the seed, and the verdict is repeated from the stored observation without AI. A module runs by the format version that its module manifest names, and a new format version does not change its verdict until the author moves the module to it;
- a module's version is semver `X.Y.Z`. An incompatible change — removing a skill or changing its meaning, changing the key of a published task — raises the major version and carries `retired_skills`. Moving a module to a new format version is a new version of the module too;
- the module index is cumulative: it names each published version of each module by its package digest, with the tag of the release in whose files the package lies, and with the withdrawal mark `withdrawn` — `wrong_key`, `answer_leak` or `unsuitable_content`. A withdrawal is marked by the index of the next release, and it takes effect with the pull request that moves `api` to this release (the text of the module format, [spec/format/README.md](../spec/format/README.md));
- the format version window: while the format is v0, the platform reads only its current version, the one of the tag of the `spec` submodule; from v1, the current major and the previous one. While the format is v0, modules move to a new version all at once, and `api` and `app` come out together;
- the engine chooses by the skill graphs of the connected modules, and the graphs are given to it and to `learner-model` by `api`. A learner connects a module, and the module's skills enter their model;
- from stage 4 widget bundles are pinned to an attempt too: a bundle is pinned by hash in the module package. A withdrawn version withdraws them too.

**The current version of a module:**
- The current version of a module is the newest by semver entry of the pinned index, if:
  - the index has not marked it `withdrawn`. A mark with any reason, an unknown one too, `api` counts as a withdrawal;
  - its `module_format_version` is in the window of `api`. An entry outside the window the choice skips, and the index does not reject it (the text of the module format).

  This is the same rule by which the format chooses the version of a module that teaches an anchor skill.
- The current version is one for all learners. It changes with a pull request that moves the configuration to another release of `modules`, or with a change of the window — the move of `api` to a new line of `spec`, — and takes effect with the build of the image.
- A new attempt outside a lesson is on the current version. A task of a lesson is an attempt on the package on which the lesson began: the lesson holds its digest, and all its tasks go on this package. `api` leads the lesson by a run on this package.
- A module's connection is the module ID and the date of connection, without a version (the table of what lies where). `engine` gets the graphs of the current versions of the connected modules that suit the learner, and `learner-model` the graphs of the current versions of all the modules whose evidence is in the profile's log, connected or not.
- Whether a module suits a learner — by audience, and a module is not selected by explanation language: the profile has no languages of the learner, and a task and a lesson by age categories, — `api` judges by the current version. If it does not suit the learner, while the previous one did, for this learner the module is like a module without a current version: `api` does not give them the previous version, and the version is still one for all. An open lesson run is otherwise: the module is still by the current version, and the lesson and each of its tasks by the run's package, the one the learner sees.
- A module without a current version: all its versions in the window are withdrawn, or the window moved forward before `modules` released a version in it. It stays connected, its evidence is in the log, but the engine does not get it, and there are no new attempts and lessons on it. It gives no graph to `learner-model` either, so its evidence waits until the module has a current version. This is only when the module has no current version at all. A module whose current version does not suit one learner is, for this learner, without new attempts and lessons and without a graph at the engine, but `learner-model` gets its graph. `app` gets such a module in the list of connected ones with the mark "no current version", without a digest: the module is visible, it can be disconnected, and there are no attempts and lessons on it.
- **The deployment's list of modules**: `API_CATALOG_MODULES`, the IDs separated by commas, which `infra` sets for each deployment; unset or empty, the whole pinned index. An ID the pinned index does not hold is a failed start of `serve` and a refusal of `api check`. A module the list leaves out keeps its current version, but is offered no more, as a module without one for this learner is: the catalog does not give it, its connection is refused "no current version", connected already it comes without a digest, the engine does not get it, and a new attempt, a new lesson run and its skill map are refused "no current version". A run or an attempt already open on it goes on to its end on its package — the full check of a run reads the current version, which the list does not take away —, and `learner-model` still gets its graph, as for a module whose current version does not suit one learner.

**What a transition changes** — a new release of `modules` in the configuration or a new format window of `api`. Each transition changes the cache mark, and the state of each learner is recomputed from the log at the first reading. Beyond that:

| What is in the transition | New attempt | Begun attempt and lesson | Learner model | Files and open views |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a patch or minor version of a module | on the new version | go on their own package to the end | — | of the previous version — by its digest, as before |
| a major version with `retired_skills` | on the new version | go on their own package to the end | carry-over by `retired_skills` at recomputation and at appending; evidence on the previous version `learner-model` appends with the same carry-over | of the previous version — as before |
| withdrawal `wrong_key` | on the newest version not withdrawn | are closed "unchecked" | the evidence of the withdrawn version is cut off by the filter at recomputation | refusal |
| withdrawal `answer_leak` or `unsuitable_content` | on the newest version not withdrawn | are closed "unchecked" | the evidence remains | refusal |
| withdrawal of the newest major version, and a version of the previous major becomes current | on the version of the previous major | are closed "unchecked" | the evidence of the withdrawn major about skills that are not in the graph of the current version has nothing to be carried over by: `retired_skills` maps only earlier IDs to new ones. It waits in the log, goes nowhere and is not seen on the map until the next version names those skills | refusal |
| a format version that `api` does not read yet | the entry is skipped, the previous version stays current | go on their own package to the end | — | — |
| `api` moves to a new line of `spec` with a new format version | on a version of the new format version | the packages of the previous format version leave the image, and the attempts and lessons on them are closed "unchecked" | moving a module to a new format version is a new version of the module, and if it is major, the carry-over is as in the row of the major version. A new format version may also change the anchor skills of the shared taxonomy, and their mastery is derived by `learner-model` | the previous packages are gone |

The filter `wrong_key` reads `withdrawn` on each entry of the index, in place, and on the entries outside the window too: they are skipped only by the choice of the current version (the text of the module format).

A rollback to the previous major is an extreme case. A withdrawn newest major is fixed by `modules` with a version above it by semver — not necessarily of the same major: a fix of the key of a published task is itself major. The fix comes out in the same release that withdraws the version: a version that comes out in this same release can be withdrawn too. Then the fix becomes current, not the previous major, and it names each skill of the withdrawn one in `skills` or `retired_skills`.

**Closing attempts on a withdrawn version and outside the window:**
- They are closed by an operator job with the image of the new release, after the deployment: it finds the open attempts and lesson runs whose packages the new image does not give — on a withdrawn version or outside the window. So no open attempts on packages that are not in the image remain in the database.
- The check on request is mandatory, not only a safety net: an attempt or a lesson run whose package `api` no longer gives it closes the same way at the first request about them. Between the deployment and the job only this check closes such attempts, and a package that has left the window is no longer in the image. Abandoned attempts are closed by the operator's daily retention job, and it is also the safety net for these.
- While a deployment is going on, requests may be served by the previous image too. The job runs when the new image has taken all requests. An attempt on a version that the new release withdrew may still be begun by the previous image, and the check on request of the new image will close it. A cache mark newer than its own — by release or window — the previous image does not overwrite: it computes the state for the answer without writing it.
- "No longer gives" means that the image's own index has marked the version withdrawn or that its format version is outside this image's window. A package digest that the image's index does not know — this is the previous image during a deployment and a package that the new release brought — gets a temporary refusal without a record, and a repeat of the request will reach the new image. Otherwise the previous image would close the attempts and lesson runs of the new one "unchecked". The index is cumulative, so the new image knows the digests of all the previous ones. A rollback to an image older than the release on which attempts were begun is not allowed: `api` is rolled back only by rolling forward, and until then such attempts and runs get a temporary refusal and wait.
- The verdict is "unchecked" with a neutral phrase: this is not the learner's error. The words of hints are deleted in the same transaction. The evidence is the outcome "unchecked", its weight is decided by `learner-model`, and with `wrong_key` the filter cuts it off. An attempt of a task with a rubric has no evidence here either.
- In code the job is the command `api after`, which the deployment of `infra` calls with the role `after`. It reads only the database of `api`, through the pooler, and the image's packages, and closes each attempt and each run in a transaction of its own, in the order of the request: account, run, attempt. The evidence is written in the same transaction by the catch-up of owed evidence, and an attempt on a package outside the window gets none (Д27). A digest that the image's index does not know the job does not touch. The same two steps run in the daily `api sweep` before the closing of abandoned ones, so the safety net closes such attempts "unchecked", not "abandoned".

**The image:**
- holds all the versions of the pinned index whose format version is in the window of `api`, withdrawn ones too: a withdrawn one stays only for repeating a verdict. Which versions to hold is said by the index, and the build needs no database. Thereby the image holds all the versions in the window to which unfinished attempts and lessons are pinned, and attempts on packages that have left the window are closed as said above;
- a package leaves the image when its format version leaves the window. While the format is v0, this is each new format version, and modules move to it all at once;
- the server part lies by the package digest, and each file of the device part by its own digest, once: an unchanged media file of a new version takes no space;
- a repeat of a verdict by a version outside the window happens outside `api`, and the package is taken from the release of `modules`;
- the image's size is printed by the job "Image" of CI; its measurements without packages and with them are measurements 1.3 and 1.4.

**Configuration** — the file `modules.json` at the root of the repository:

```json
{
  "release": "vX.Y.Z",
  "index": "sha256:<64 hexadecimal digits>"
}
```

- `release` is the tag of a release of `modules`, `vX.Y.Z`, as any NovaTrail repository numbers its releases. `index` is the digest of the bytes of the file `module-index.json` of this release: `sha256:` and 64 lowercase characters, like a digest in the format (the text of the module format, [spec/format/README.md](../spec/format/README.md)). The index is a canonical text, so this is also the digest of its canonical text.
- The file lists no modules: a learner has access to the modules of the index that suit them by audience, and a module is not selected by explanation language: the profile has no languages of the learner. It holds no package digests: the index names them, and the chain from it holds each file.
- `api` embeds the file in the binary by a Go package at the root of the module, beside the file: a `//go:embed` pattern is read from the folder of its own package and does not accept `..` ([`embed`](https://pkg.go.dev/embed@go1.27.3) of Go 1.27.3). There is no copy of the file: CI checks the same file that is embedded, and the image build reads that same file. It is changed by a pull request of the author.

**Check in CI** — a job of its own in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, beside the shared ones, in the stage `toolchain` of the container, with network:
1. takes from the release of `altnovatrail/modules` with the tag `release` the files `module-index.json` and `module-index.json.sigstore.json` — by the public address, without a token, as the shared release takes the previous index for a release of `modules`;
2. checks the signature: `just -f spec/justfile release-verify modules <release> module-index.json`. The recipe accepts only a signature of the shared `release.yml` of `.github`, of a version no lower than the one it names, on the tag's commit, and without network or Sigstore rejects the file;
3. checks the file's SHA-256 against `index`;
4. rejects a `release` older than the one pinned on `main`: a withdrawal is marked only by the index of later releases, and the old index does not have it;
5. rejects a pull request after which a module that has a current version on `main` would lose it otherwise than by a withdrawal: for example, `api` moves to a new line of `spec`, and `modules.json` stays on a release without packages of the new format version. Only a withdrawal may leave a module without a current version;
6. prints the current version of each module: in a pull request the file changes only the tag, and the author sees what learners will get.

The job runs on each pull request and push to `main`, not only when the file has changed. A required check whose workflow is skipped by a path filter stays pending and blocks the merge (GitHub documentation, sources 4.2), and a new tag of `spec` may raise the minimum version of the shared `release.yml` that `release-verify` accepts. Making the job a required check is a change of the ruleset, a step of the author. In code this is the job "Modules" of `ci.yml`: `.github/modules.sh` in the stage `toolchain`, with the submodule and the history of `main`, calls `just ci-modules` — the index and the signature by the public address, `release-verify`, then `go run ./cmd/modules-check`, a program outside the release binary, by items 3–6.

**Scheduled withdrawal** (sources 4.4) — the job "Withdrawals", `.github/workflows/withdrawals.yml`, once a day at 05:17 UTC and by hand from the Actions tab, in the stage `toolchain-core`, which runs no test:
1. takes the tag of the newest release of `modules` — by the redirect of the public address `releases/latest`, without a token. A tag lower than the pinned one is replaced by the pinned one;
2. takes its index and signature and checks the signature by `release-verify` on each run, even when the newest is the pinned one;
3. takes the index of the pinned release — the same file when the newest is the pinned one — and checks it against `index`;
4. `modules-check -latest` joins the withdrawals of the newest index with the pinned one — `moduleindex.WithLatest` of `module-kit`, — prints each version that the pinned index gives (in the window and not withdrawn) and the newest withdraws, with the reason and a mark of the current version, and then the job fails. An index that does not hold the entries of the pinned one — the index of an older release — is a refusal too.

A failure is a signal to the author: GitHub sends the notice of a scheduled job to whoever last changed its cron line. The job fails every day until `modules.json` pins a release with the mark. It has no rights beyond reading, requires no repository settings, is not a required check and does not run on pull requests. In the recipe — `just ci-withdrawals`; `.github/modules.sh` calls it the same way as `ci-modules`.

**Image build.** The image is built by the deployment of `infra`: it takes from the release of `api` the signed binary and the `Dockerfile`, checks them by `release-verify` and builds the image by this `Dockerfile`, on `gcr.io/distroless/static-debian13:nonroot` by digest; puts it into the closed Artifact Registry of `infra` and rolls out a Cloud Run revision by the image's digest. NovaTrail publishes no images. The deployment builds the stage `release`: it puts into the runtime the release binary from its build context `binary`, and the last stage builds the same image from source code — the job "Image" of CI builds and checks it. The module packages are put into the image by the code of `api`, not by the deployment: the build stage of the `Dockerfile` calls a command of the `api` binary, which reads the embedded `modules.json` and does the following:
1. takes `module-index.json` of the release `release` and checks its SHA-256 against `index`. The signature is not checked a second time: from here on the file is held by the digest that the checker pinned;
2. for each entry in the window takes the package files from the release that its `release` names, by the layout of a release of `modules`. The name of the package file is derived from its index entry, without listing the release's files and without the REST API: the package lies as an archive `sha256-<hex>.tar.gz` — the entry's `package_digest` with `-` in place of `:`, — at the release's public address, without a token;
3. `module-kit` checks the inventory against `package_digest`, the parts against the inventory, and the files of the device part against its list;
4. puts the packages into the image, as said above.

Any mismatch is a build refusal. The build needs network. In code this is the command `api packages <folder>` and the stages `release-packages` and `source-packages`: the binary lays the packages out in `/tmp/packages`, and the image stage copies the folder to `/packages` with root as owner. The archive is unpacked in memory, strictly: only regular files and the folders `device/`, only the package's paths, without links, sparse files and `..`, with size limits. The folder holds `module-index.json`, `packages/sha256-<hex>/` with the three texts of the package, and `files/sha256-<hex>` — a file of the device part under its own digest. In the image there are only the binary and the packages, without a shell and not as root, so migrations and operator jobs are commands of the `api` binary too. The format of the rollout list, what the deployment checks before deploying, and whether it rejects a release of `api` lower than the one rolled out, is `infra`'s. A rollback of `api` to an image with an old release of `modules` would bring back withdrawn versions, so `api` is rolled back only by rolling forward.

**Start and serving:**
- at start `api` checks the SHA-256 of the index in the image against `index` of the embedded configuration. A package is checked by `module-kit` by the chain from the index when `api` reads it. In code, `serve` reads the folder `API_PACKAGES_DIR`, by default `/packages`, before it opens the databases: the texts of packages by the chain of `module-kit`, the files of the device parts by size under their own digest; a foreign index or a broken package is a start failure; the line `startup` names the release of `modules` and the number of packages;
- the files of the device part and the open views `api` serves by package digest and only for the versions that the image gives. For a withdrawn one or one outside the window, a refusal: for a file, `410`, for an open view, the cause "version withdrawn". `app` learns of a withdrawal by a request of its own — "which of these digests does the image no longer give", — at launch and after such a refusal, and deletes their open views from its cache: it takes open views from the cache and does not reach `api` with them. In code this is `internal/catalog`: the status of a digest in the image's index — "gives", "no longer gives" or "does not know" — decides the answer of open views, `CheckPackages` and the file paths, and the current version of a module for a learner is decided by `moduleindex.Newest` and the audience of its module manifest;
- a withdrawal reaches learners only when the author moves the configuration to a release with the mark. While there is no pull request, the withdrawn version stays current, but not silently: the job "Withdrawals" fails every day while the newest release withdraws a version that the image gives.

**The first app:**
- the same `modules.json` and the same path: a `just` recipe calls the same command as the image build stage and puts the packages into a folder outside git. There are no learners' data in the packages;
- tests of code run without network, on the package and index vectors of `spec` — `format/vectors/packages/` and `format/vectors/indexes/`, — which the Go code takes from the Go module `spec`, `spec.Format`, of the format's current version;
- which modules the pinned release holds, and their current versions, the job "Modules" prints: among them `english-essays`, with tasks with a rubric, `grade-3-math`, on a workbook whose cells the code does not begin yet (Д24), and `check-board`, the tasks of `tutor`'s board on a handwriting sheet, for adults, which a deployment at the rung `full` refuses "not admitted". The test of the job "Modules" walks the path of the first app on the packages, and the owner's path on the board module in the dev mode. The recipe is `just packages`, by default into the folder `/home/vscode/.novatrail-dev/packages` of the dev volume, and `serve` takes it as `API_PACKAGES_DIR`.

**What `api` expects from `module-kit`:** reading packages of the format's current version and checking them by the chain from the index that a pull request of `api` pinned. These are the Go packages of `module-kit` at the version of `go.mod`: `modulepackage` reads a package by the chain of digests, `moduleindex` reads the index and the version of a new attempt, and `observation` does the reading join and the verdict. The newest version that is not withdrawn, for a new attempt, is chosen by a function of the `module-kit` library — its command `open` uses the same one, — and the rules of version comparison are in the text of the module format, [spec/format/README.md](../spec/format/README.md): in 0.y.z, y plays the major, and while the format is v0, a module's move to a new module format version is a major change.

### 3.4. Carrying progress over after a major version of a module

The facts about `learner-model` and `module-kit` the design rests on are in their plans, [RUN.md](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/9f338ae/RUN.md) and [docs/architecture.md](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/9f338ae/docs/architecture.md) of `learner-model` at 9f338ae and [RUN.md of `module-kit`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/module-kit/blob/1a54028/RUN.md) at 1a54028.

**What the decision rests on:**
- a published skill ID never names another skill: a skill whose meaning changed gets a new ID. Skill IDs lie in the publisher's namespace, and outside a module a skill is named by its full ID — the module, the kind and the local ID (the text of the module format, [spec/format/README.md](../spec/format/README.md));
- progress survives a module update — a principle of the platform;
- the table of correspondence is `retired_skills` of the skill graph: the skills of earlier versions under their earlier IDs, and each has `replaced_by` — the skills of this version that took what it taught. A renamed skill has one skill in `replaced_by`, a split one has several, and skills merged into one each name that one. `[]` says that the progress of a retired skill goes nowhere. The record stays in all following versions, and if a skill from `replaced_by` is later retired itself, the record names its replacements instead. So the table of the current version carries over progress of any age without the old graphs (the text of the module format);
- `retired_skills` is closed, and its record has only `replaced_by`, without a title: `tiers` and `skills` are open, and the map is drawn by them (the schema `skill-graph.schema.json`);
- the server carries progress over: the evidence of a retired skill goes to each skill that replaced it, and the weight is a decision of `learner-model`. That each skill of the earlier version is in the new one or in `retired_skills` is checked by `module-kit` when it compares versions. Across a move of format v0 there is no check of skills: the CLI checks only that the version grew, and the graph is not changed in the move — the review of `modules` holds this;
- a module refers to the skills of other modules only through anchor skills of the shared taxonomy with the ID `core.<name>`: renaming a skill in another module breaks nothing. An anchor skill can also be a supporting skill of a task (the schema `task.schema.json`, `supporting`). The vocabulary of anchor skills changes by format versions, not module versions, and `learner-model` derives the mastery of an anchor skill from the skills that teach it;
- a trap ID is not reused either, but traps have no table of correspondence, and the skills of a trap are named by the catalog of the version (the text of the module format);
- the evidence log is the truth, and the state is a cache, which is recomputed when the version of `learner-model`, the release of `modules` or the format's window changes. The current version of a module is one for all learners and changes with a deployment of the image.

**When and over what.** Carrying over is not a separate step but part of each computation of the state from the log. There is no separate job or migration, and the log does not change:
1. A pull request moves `modules.json` to a release where a major version of the module became current, and the image with it is rolled out. The cache mark no longer matches the one the image runs on.
2. The first read of a profile's model — the engine choice after an attempt, the skill map, the export — recomputes the state. `api` reads the profile's log, cuts off the `wrong_key` evidence by package digest and gives `learner-model` the log and the graphs of the current versions of all the log's modules with their `retired_skills`, in the types of `learner-model`.
3. `learner-model` routes each piece of evidence by the rule below and gives the state.
4. `api` writes the state into the cache with the new mark and the number of the last evidence counted, under a lock of the cache's row. A mark newer than its own it does not overwrite.
5. To the engine `api` gives this state and the graphs of the current versions of the connected modules that suit the learner. The graph of a connected module about which there is no evidence yet `learner-model` gets too, but it does not go into the cache (see "Repeatability"). The choice stores the digests of all the graphs that `learner-model` and the engine got, the release of `modules` and the number of the last evidence.
6. New evidence `learner-model` appends to the cache by the same rule, and the evidence of an attempt begun on the earlier version of the module too: it names the skills of the earlier version. The number of the evidence is the next after the last in the log, and the tail of the log that the cache has not counted yet `api` appends to the cache first — both at a read and before an append.

**The rule by skill ID.** `learner-model` looks up each module skill ID in a piece of evidence in the graph of the current version of the module that this ID names:

| Skill ID in the evidence | Where the evidence goes |
|---|---|
| the skill is in `skills` of the current version | to it: the meaning of a skill under the same ID does not change |
| the skill is in `retired_skills` of the current version | to each skill from `replaced_by`, with the weight that `learner-model` decides; with `[]` — to none |
| the skill is in neither — a new skill of a withdrawn major, a skill of a module with no current version, a gap in the table | to no skill, without an error. The evidence waits in the log until the graph of the current version names the skill |
| an anchor skill `core.<name>` that the vocabulary of some format version in the window of `api` knows | as it is: it has no module, and `retired_skills` does not concern it |
| an anchor skill that the vocabulary no longer knows | to no skill: there is no table for anchor skills, and a new meaning comes under a new ID |

The skills of a trap, if the evidence carries them, go by the same rule. The trap ID itself is stable, and a trap that is not in the catalog of the current version is not among the repeating traps: there is no description for it. What this needs from `learner-model` are the conditions of carrying over that `spec` sets for it.

**What the learner sees on the skill map** after carrying over — the graph of the current version and the state computed from the log:

| What the major version did | On the map |
|---|---|
| renamed a skill | the new skill with the evidence of the earlier one |
| split a skill | each part with the evidence of the earlier skill |
| merged skills | one skill with the evidence of all |
| retired a skill without a pair, `replaced_by: []` | there is no skill. Its evidence stays in the log and goes into the export under the earlier full ID |
| added a skill | a skill without evidence |

With what weight the evidence goes to the new skills and whether "mastered" carries over, `learner-model` decides. There is no notice to the learner or the adult. The data of the map for `skill-map` is the state of the skills of the current version, and the graph and the tiers `app` takes by the digest of the current version, so a skill without a pair and a waiting skill are not on the map.

**Withdrawal of the newest major.** When a version of the previous major becomes current, the evidence about skills that are in it under the same ID goes to them as usual. About skills it does not know, the evidence waits: it stays in the log, goes nowhere and is not visible on the map. When the next version names such a skill in `skills` or `retired_skills`, its release changes the cache mark, and the recomputation counts the evidence. With `wrong_key` the filter cuts it off anyway. It comes back only if version comparison counts the withdrawn version as published: its skill IDs are not reused, and a version above it by semver names each of its skills in `skills` or `retired_skills`. So the format has it, and the fix of a withdrawn newest major is a version above it by semver, in the same release that withdraws it: it names the skills of the withdrawn one, and they hardly have to wait.

**Repeatability.** The same prefix of the log up to number N, the same `wrong_key` marks of the pinned index, the same graphs of the current versions and the same version of `learner-model` give the same carrying over, and the cache mark and the number of the last evidence counted hold them. The engine choice stores the digests of the graphs, the release and the number, so carrying over repeats in a repeat of the choice from the log too. In the cache is only what the log, the release, the window and the version of `learner-model` define: the state of the skills of the log's modules. What depends on connections and the profile — the starting point of skills without evidence, including in a connected module about which there is no evidence yet, and the mastery of an anchor skill by the skills "in the learner's modules" (the text of the module format), — `learner-model` computes at a read, by the graphs that `api` gives at that time, and does not write into the cache. Time is not carrying over either: forgetting is `learner-model`'s. If any of this goes into the cache, Т-22 comes back. A snapshot of the state, if `learner-model` keeps one, holds only under the same mark, and carrying over does not apply to it — the log is carried over, not what was computed.

**The lesson and the export:**
- a lesson begun on an earlier package names the skills of the earlier version: they go to the engine translated by the same rule, and `api` takes them from the package of the lesson run;
- its own part of the export names, for each module of the log, the version by whose graph the state is computed, and for a module with no current version, that there is no graph and its evidence waits. The log carries the earlier IDs, and the table of an official module a reader will find in the open release of that version. How this goes for third-party modules, of stage 4, is decided together with their licenses (О-10). The export format is `spec`'s, `export/`.

**What `retired_skills` does not express** — and this needs no row «в реестр spec». There is no moving a skill to another module: modules are linked only by anchor skills, so a skill is retired in one module and appears as new in another. The outcome here is as with `[]`. A version without a skill graph cannot carry `retired_skills`: without a graph a module has no skills (the text of the module format). Its evidence goes by the rule "in neither": it waits in the log, and a version with a graph that names its skills in `skills` or `retired_skills` brings it back.

**In the code** (`internal/model`). The graphs of the current versions are built by `learnermodel.NewGraph` at start — the bands, the skills with assessability, anchor skills, prerequisites and tier, `retired_skills` and the descriptions of tasks, — and a module with no current version gets "no current version", and its evidence waits. `learner-model` does the carrying over both at a recomputation and at an append, and the log keeps the earlier IDs: the evidence of an attempt on 1.0.0 about a skill that 2.0.0 renamed lands on the new skill, and the earlier ID stays in the log — a test checks this. The data of the skill map is `GetSkillMap`: the state of the skills of the current version with assessability `verdict` and of the anchor skills that the graph names, and for an anchor skill also the modules that teach it and are not connected to the profile.

Rejected: never to show, as in canvas-api ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs)), and to count the task as skipped without changing the rating, as in the prototype;
- the ladder goes by the number of wrong answers on the task, the program chooses the rung, and the model only finds the words; after the third rung the program offers to call an adult — to a child — or to show the solution. A task's solution in a module is closed and required for a task that has an item with a verdict;
- the key and the solution of a task go to the device only when the attempt is closed by a correct answer or by showing the solution, and an attempt that was left, abandoned or closed "unchecked" does not give the key. The keys of open modules are public, and holding the key back does not guard a secret but only keeps it out of the learner's app;
- the outcomes of evidence are from the first submission, after hint number k, with a sketch hint, solution shown, unchecked; their weights are decided by `learner-model`;
- О-21: before the first wrong answer a request gives "givens and unknown" and the task's technique — without AI and without reading the work, — and there is no sketch "how to begin" in format v0. After a wrong answer — the current rung, and "explain differently" — the same rung in other words, an AI call within the quota, with the previous questions of the same task;
- the work is read only by the learner's action, and a request does not send unsubmitted work for reading. `app` sends a request for a hint without the work, and the request always goes to `api` ([a42a7e1](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/blob/a42a7e1/docs/architecture.md));
- the fallback text is, on rungs 1–2, the text of the trap value, without it the trap advice, without a trap the module's rung template; on the third rung, the item's simpler case, and without it the same as on the second. When a submission has several wrong answer slots, it speaks about the first answer slot "not yet" in order;
- any text of the tutor passes the leak check, and a hint also the form check, before it goes to the device; if it did not pass, the learner gets the fallback text. Of what is stored about attempts, only the previous questions of the same task go to AI — for the hint ladder;
- in the solving mode "by steps" the app leads the learner by the solving steps, in the solving mode "self" the tutor waits until it is called. "Step done" does not read the work and leads to the next step on the device, without AI. `app` asks for the words of a rung after the verdict "not yet" and sends the work for them once more.

**The ladder.** A wrong answer is a submission in which at least one answer slot got "not yet". "Unreadable" and "unchecked" do not raise the rung: it is not the learner's error. An attempt stores the number of wrong answers as a whole.

| Wrong answer | Rung | Words | What else `app` offers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1 — a general question about the place of the error | an AI call; if it did not come or did not pass the checks — the fallback text | "explain differently" |
| 2nd | 2 — a question about the rule or the trap | the same | "explain differently" |
| 3rd | 3 — a simpler case | the same; the fallback text is the item's simpler case | "explain differently" |
| 4th and on | no new one | no new words, and AI is not called for them; "Hint" gives the words of rung 3 | to a child — call an adult or show the solution; to a learner of the category `adult` — show the solution; "explain differently" of rung 3, if it was not asked for yet |

From the fourth wrong answer — as `offer_help` in canvas-api, where the analysis went on only while there were no more than three wrong answers ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs)). The ladder is per task, not per item: on a workbook page four submissions with an error offer to show the solution of the whole page. A task with a rubric has no ladder: its item has no verdict, and it goes the path of a task with a rubric, below.

**Showing the solution.** A child is a profile of any age category but `adult`:
1. Before the fourth wrong answer there is no showing: `api` rejects a request to show by any kind of action.
2. From the fourth wrong answer `app` offers to call an adult or to show the solution. "Call an adult" is only an interface of `app`: `api` notifies nobody.
3. If the setting "ask before showing" is on — and by default it is on, — `app` asks for the PIN of the section for adults and sends the showing as an adult action with the profile's ID, the right `can_manage`. A learning request to show `api` rejects with the cause "an adult is needed". If the adult turned the setting off, showing is a learning action, `can_learn`.
4. `api`, in one transaction, closes the attempt with the reason "solution shown", writes the evidence, deletes the words of the hints and gives the task's solution. The answer says what comes next: outside a lesson — the engine choice, and in a lesson — the place of the next segment or "no more segments", and the next segment is begun by a request of `app`.

A learner of the category `adult`:
1. Showing is from the start of the attempt, as a learning action, `can_learn`; they have no setting.
2. From the fourth wrong answer `app` offers to show the solution.
3. Closing is as for a child, step 4.

A request to show carries no proof that the PIN was asked for: on a shared device the token is one, and the server tells kinds of actions apart, not people. The refusal "an adult is needed" is not a security boundary but one place where the setting is enforced for all devices of the account. The setting is changed by an adult by an action on the profile, `can_manage`. By which kind of action the solution was shown `api` does not store.

A correct answer — all items of the task are correct — closes the attempt too, and the solution comes together with the verdict. The tutor's words after it — praise and a question about which technique helped — `app` asks for the same way, by a second request, and `api` gives them on a closed attempt too: once — the attempt marks that they were given, — and while the profile has not begun a new attempt. The words themselves `api` does not store, and this call does not get the previous questions: they were deleted with the words of the hints.

**A hint on request:**

| When | What "Hint" gives | AI |
|---|---|---|
| before the first wrong answer — and after submissions that had only "unreadable" or "unchecked" | the task's technique — the IDs of the techniques, empty if there are none, — and `api` records that it gave it on request. "Givens and unknown" `app` shows from the task's open view, and they are not recorded. Also the task's leading question, `leading_question` of the attempt's instance, if it has one: the field `GetHintResponse.leading_question`; an open attempt holds the one given in `OpenAttempt.requested_leading_question`, and `api` records that it gave it only when it is not empty | no |
| after a wrong answer | the words of the current rung that the attempt stores; if there are none — the second request for them did not arrive, — the fallback text of the rung. The leading question beside the words of a rung is always empty: after the first wrong answer it is no longer given | no |
| "explain differently" after a wrong answer | the same rung in other words, once per rung | one call, within the quota |

A request does not raise the rung: it grows only with wrong answers. "Hint" in `app` is always there, in both modes, from the start of the task, and it sends no work. At a solving step whose `gives` gives techniques, the technique goes the same way: `app` names the attempt and the step's place, `api` answers with the IDs of the task's techniques and records that it gave it at the step. A request and the start of a step are two requests, because the evidence tells a technique at a step from one on request.

**"Explain differently":**
- the model gets the same as for the words of a rung, but for the work: the key and the solution of the task, the trap of the rung's answer slot, the rung, the presentation style, the explanation language, the age category and the AI route, — and the task's previous questions. The trap — the program's finding by the verdict — the words of a rung get too, in the second request, when the verdict is already recorded. The model does not get what was read, just as the words of a rung do not, and does not get the work because the request does not send it;
- the answer passes the leak check and the form check. If it did not come or did not pass, the learner gets the fallback text of the rung, and if they are reading exactly that text, the outcome "no other words" in a successful answer, without a repeat: the call was made, and the right to "differently" is spent;
- the right to "differently" is spent as soon as the call has gone, however it ended, so there are no more than three calls beyond the words of the rungs per attempt. With an exhausted quota or without an AI route there is no call, and the right stays;
- a second time on the same rung `api` rejects with a cause, and `app` keeps the earlier words;
- without AI — a tapped choice in the mode without AI (О-5) — "explain differently" is not offered: the fallback texts remain.

**The words of a rung:**
- each call of an attempt's words — both of a rung and of "differently" — gets the previous questions of the same task, so as not to repeat them, as in canvas-api ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs));
- an AI failure, an exhausted quota and words that did not pass the leak check or the form check give the fallback text of the rung: the verdict is already given, and the learner always gets an answer. A neutral phrase is only for the verdict "unchecked";
- **the rung's answer slot** is the first answer slot "not yet" in order in the submission that gave the rung: items by place, answer slots by order in the item. The program chooses it, and everything else depends on it: the words of a rung and of "differently" get its trap, the fallback text of rungs 1–2 is the text of its trap value, without it the trap advice of its trap, without a trap the rung template, and on the third, the simpler case of its item. From the fourth wrong answer there is no rung, and the answer slot stays the one that rung 3 speaks about: "Hint", the fallback text and "differently" speak about one place. `api` does not store the answer slot: it is derived from the verdicts of the submission.

**The end of an attempt.** A profile has one open attempt: a new one — by a lesson, by the engine choice or the same task anew — closes the previous one in the same transaction, and a constraint of the database does not let a second open one in, so two devices of one profile do not open two attempts at once. Beside it go the open attempts of one page from paper: `BeginPaperAttempts` begins one for each task picked and closes another page's "left" in the same transaction, by `page_id`, and a start of a module closes none of them. In the code an attempt has its kind, `module` or `paper`, of the migration `0022`, whose checks keep the module, the package, the task and the seed on an attempt of a module and none on one of paper; the constraint is the unique partial index `attempts (profile_id) WHERE closed_at IS NULL AND kind = 'module'`, and an abandoned attempt is closed by the step `abandoned_attempts` of the job `api sweep`. The start, the open attempt, the submission of a tapped choice, the start of a step and "Done" are run by the package `internal/learning`: a start closes the open attempt in the same transaction — "left", and an overdue one "unchecked" or "abandoned", — and a request on an attempt with no actions for 7 days closes it "abandoned". An action on an attempt is a start, a submission and the start of a step, and reading the open attempt is not an action. While the attempt is open, the learner comes back to it from another device too, though the strokes stayed on the earlier one.

| Close reason | When | Outcome of the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "correct" | all items are correct | from the first submission or after hint number k |
| "solution shown" | showing the solution | "solution shown", that is "not solved" by О-7, without its own trap |
| "left" | the profile began another attempt | there were wrong answers — the outcome "left unfinished"; there were none — there is no evidence |
| "abandoned" | 7 days without actions: a check at a request and the operator's daily job of retention periods | the same as for "left" |
| "unchecked" | the version was withdrawn or left the window | "unchecked"; an attempt with a rubric has no evidence |
| "not suitable" | the lesson run ended "not suitable" while its attempt was open | the same as for "left" |
| "done" | the learner finished an attempt of a task with a rubric — after at least one submission | there is no evidence, as for any attempt with a rubric (О-17) |

- Closing always goes in one transaction: the reason, the evidence if there is any, and deleting the words of the hints. The job for abandoned ones is also a safety net for the attempts of a version that was withdrawn or left the window, and a closing by the job does not move the profile's activity date.
- In the code "in one transaction" holds the closing in a request and the evidence: the closing calls the catch-up of owed evidence, which appends the evidence of the profile's closed attempts. An attempt that the job `api sweep` closed in a batch gets its evidence from the job's next step `evidence`, and an attempt closed by the previous image, from the first catch-up of its profile. The evidence carries a leading question on request in a field of its own, kind 2, and an answer slot of the kind `time` as a kind of its own, as the schema of `learner-model` knows them.
- An attempt that was left or abandoned without wrong answers stays a row with a close reason: an adult sees it in progress, as a skip in standalone ([89986bc](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/tree/89986bc/docs)), and the learner model does not.
- The outcome "left unfinished" is a term of the platform's glossary. The name is not "not solved": that is what О-7 calls the value "solution shown".
- If the solution of this task has already gone to the profile's device — after a correct answer or showing, — and it is not a template, the evidence of a new attempt carries that too: the learner knows the key, and the engine does not give such a task again at once — as the next task, and after that the engine's pause holds it. When the solution last went to the device the engine learns from the summary of issues. This is derived from the earlier attempts, and the weight is decided by `learner-model`.
- In a lesson a segment is ended by "correct" and "solution shown", and the segment of a task with a rubric by "done"; "left" and "abandoned" do not end a segment, and on return it begins a new attempt, and "unchecked" and "not suitable" end the lesson run too. Leaving the lesson does not end the run.

**Evidence.** The outcome is by the wrong answers. A request and "explain differently" do not change it, and the evidence carries the task's technique — at a step or on request — and how many times the learner asked for "differently". A leading question that was given the evidence carries in a field of its own of the schema of `learner-model`, `leading_question_on_request`, separately from the technique on request: the log is append-only, and a question recorded in the technique's field would mix two facts forever. The field belongs to evidence of kind 2, and evidence of kind 1 has none. That the learner saw "givens and unknown" is not recorded: in the solving mode "by steps" `app` shows them at a step without a request to `api`. On showing the solution and on leaving, the evidence carries the traps that the program found in the wrong answers, and the items correct to the end, with their verdict. The weights are decided by `learner-model`.

**What is stored** — the rows "profile", "attempt" and "words of hints" of the table of what the database stores: for a child, the setting "ask before showing"; for an attempt, the number of wrong answers, "differently" and the source of its words by rung, whether the correct answer words were given, the time the solution was shown, the close reason and the time of the last action.

**In the code** (`internal/learning`, `hints.go` and `ladder.go`):
- the rung is `min(wrong answers, 3)`; the rung's answer slot and its trap the code finds anew: in the answer of the submission that gave the rung, the first answer slot "not yet", and the trap of the value in it is given by `verdict.OfSlot` of `module-kit`. The fallback text is by the chain the module format sets;
- on a workbook page the rung's slot is found the same way, the first box "not yet" by the order of items and boxes, since its every answer slot is a box. The words of a rung and those after a correct answer take no work of a page — with it `invalid_argument` — and go as for a task a tap checks, by `CallHint` with no crop: the words call carries one crop of a sheet, and `tutor` has no box of the first mistake on a page (Д42 in [DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md));
- the model's words pass the leak check `leak` of `module-kit` on the attempt's instance and the form check `words.Hint` of `tutor`; if they did not pass — the fallback text without an outcome, and the line `request` names the check in the field `words_check`: `empty`, `leak` or `form`. The presentation style for the model is by the age category: the profile has nothing else that goes to AI;
- the gate admits the words by their kinds of call: with the work `CallWords`, which `tutor` admits only with the hint on the analysis's model, and without it — "explain differently" and a task a tap checks — `CallHint`. The correct answer words are the call `CorrectAnswerWords` of the kind `CallCorrect`: `CorrectWords` asks `ChooseCall(CallCorrect)`, `TellCorrectWords` calls it. `GetCorrectAnswerWords` calls it through the port `correctTeller`: by `rpc.Across`, with the reserve of `CallCorrect`, the crop of a task AI read, the keys of the task's answer slots, its solution, the titles of its techniques, the module's language and the style; the words pass `leak` and `words.Correct`, by the check `turnedDown` the ladder shares, and the attempt records them given, `attempts.correct_words_given` of the migration `0018`, which the export carries as `correct_answer_words_given`. Words that fail a check, or a call with none, give no words and leave the right. On the route to a provider the words go from the day, in UTC, that the privacy policy of the texts `api` serves is in force at version 3 or later — the legal texts of the `spec` it pins name that day — and before it are "not admitted" with no call (Д214 spec); the stub words them on any day. The path of the words call is checked by tests on stub words too;
- "Hint" before the first wrong answer is an action on the attempt: it writes when the techniques and the leading question were first given, each only when the task has it, and moves the time of the last action;
- showing the solution by an adult action checks `can_manage` in the handler: the checks before it hold only `can_learn`.

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- of what is stored, what goes to AI is the previous questions of the same task and the trap by the verdict, which the words of a rung get. The work for the words of a rung `app` sends once more, and "differently" goes without the work and without what was read;
- the new fields of an attempt — counters, marks, times and the close reason — carry no content of the work, and all of them go into the export;
- the words of the hints of an abandoned attempt live no longer than 8 days after the last action. Words and answer slots are not written into the logs of `api`.

**What the neighbours hold:**
- `tutor` — the words call `Words` with the task's previous questions and the trap of the rung's answer slot, "explain differently" without the work, the form check of a hint `tutor/words` and the call of the correct answer words;
- `learner-model` — the weights of the outcome "left unfinished" and of the facts of evidence;
- `engine` — a task whose solution the learner saw is not the next task, then a pause of 24 hours, and the second pass gives it only when the rest gave nothing;
- `app` — showing the solution, the refusal "an adult is needed" and the setting "ask before showing".

### 3.6. Platform AI quota

The provider's prices are in the record sources 7.1, the spending message of `tutor` in [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs) and [`tutor` at 9f85613](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/9f85613/docs), and the examples of MathTrail in [canvas-api at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs) and [mathtrail-standalone at 89986bc](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/tree/89986bc/docs).

**What the decision rests on:**
- `api`'s card in `spec` says that the platform AI quota `api` counts "in dollars by the spending message of `tutor`, per family day, with the caps of the profile and the account by the account's tariff", and the card of `tutor` says: "By the spending `api` counts the platform AI quotas". `tutor` reports spending in one message per library call, with a record for each model call: reading handwriting has two of them, one per reader, reading an essay has one. A record has the provider, the model, the tokens and an estimate in dollars by its price table with a date. A cut-off answer `tutor` counts by the worst case, and there is neither content nor identifiers in the message;
- platform AI has a limit — a daily quota of the tutor's help, as in the prototype, and on an own key the limit is set by the key's owner. The own key limit was decided by `tutor`: it is set at the provider, and the platform does not accumulate the spending of an own key;
- reading handwriting is two calls for each check, two readers of one provider, or one call of Haiku 5.5 · `medium` in the mode `single`, and an analysis with words is a call beyond them. A task with a rubric has reading the essay by one reader and an assessment (О-14);
- an exhausted quota is the verdict "unchecked" and a neutral phrase, not "not yet", and for the words of a rung, the fallback text;
- the mode without AI is always there, it is the free path, open to children too — the recommendation of О-5. Without AI a tapped choice is checked. An option written by hand is read by AI like any handwriting, and there is no keyboard input in format v0;
- the prototype has a free daily quota and the tariff Plus for $10 a month with a quota five times larger. The recommendation of О-14 is to decide by unit economics: a subscriber's worst month must pay for itself;
- the account holds the tariff, and the account owner's payment data from stage 3 is held by the payment provider;
- in canvas-api the counters of readings and analyses were kept per child and per parent, and the day of the limits was UTC. The reserve was taken at the start of a check, and a call that was made was counted even on a failure. An exhausted limit gave `UNCHECKED` with the cause `limit`, and the phrase promised no time ([canvas-api at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs)). The parent's counters survived the deletion of a child ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs));
- in standalone the day of the limits is the family's, by the device's clock, and a new offset takes effect from the next day. The refusal does not name the number of the limit ([mathtrail-standalone at 89986bc](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/tree/89986bc/docs)).

**The rule:**

| What | How |
|---|---|
| unit | dollars by the estimate from the spending message of `tutor`. The learner and the adult are shown a share, not a sum |
| what is counted | each model call on platform AI: both readers of handwriting, the words of a rung, "explain differently", the correct answer words, the reading and the assessment of an essay |
| own key | does not spend or reserve the quota. There is no automatic move from an own key to platform AI: it is another AI route and another handler |
| caps | per day — for the profile and for the account, by the tariff, in the table of tariffs of `internal/quota` |
| day | the family's, by the clock offset of the device; the first day by a new offset is no shorter than 20 hours |
| tariff | a field of the account: it comes with the invite code and is changed by an operator's command by the code |

- The profile's cap keeps fairness between the children of one account, the account's cap keeps spending. The owner profile spends the same cap of the account, and an account with one profile runs into the profile's cap. The values are the tariff's configuration, and a pull request of the configuration changes them, not a change of this rule.
- The quota is step 7 of the checks of a request, only for requests that call platform AI. If the account has no tariff or the tariff has no caps, platform AI is closed: "closed by default", as with the admission function.

**Family day**, as in standalone:
1. `app` sends the device's clock offset from UTC, in minutes, with requests to platform AI, and with an own key does not. An offset outside −12…+14 hours or not a multiple of a quarter of an hour `api` rejects, and the request is counted by the previous one.
2. The account holds the offset and the end of the current day. A new offset takes effect from the next day, and the current one ends at its own time. Until a device has reported an offset, the day is UTC.
3. A day runs from the family's midnight to midnight. The first day by a new offset ends at the first midnight by it that comes no earlier than 20 hours after the end of the previous day. Without this, a change of the offset from +14 hours to −12 would give a day of 2 hours and a second quota. The price of the rule: after a trip east by more than 4 hours, the first day by the new offset is longer, up to 44 hours.
4. Summer time, a trip and two devices in different time zones are an ordinary change of the offset. The offset is in no line of the log.

In the code: the function of the day is `quota.Clock` of the package `internal/quota`, pure, and the account holds the offset and the start and the end of the current day — `day_starts_at` of the migration `0014`, because the first day by a new offset may begin not at midnight, and the key of the counter is the start of the day. The offset and the day are written by `store.FamilyDay` in the first transaction of a request to platform AI — `SubmitWork` with handwriting, `SubmitEssay`, `GetRungWords`, `ExplainDifferently` and `GetCorrectAnswerWords`, — which holds the account's row anyway. A request with an own key writes no offset. Requests without an offset — the start of an attempt and "something else" — read the day by `store.Today`, writing nothing.

**Tariff and admission:**
- the operator creates an invite code by the command `api invite` with a tariff — by default "closed slice", `closed_slice`. At the first sign-in the code is bound to the account, and the tariff passes to the account;
- the account's tariff is changed by an operator's command that finds the account by its code: the account ID does not leave the platform. This is how the author and the testers get their own cap. In the code this is `api tariff -tariff <tariff> <code>`;
- the caps of each tariff are in the configuration of `api`, the table of tariffs of the package `internal/quota` that a pull request changes, in whole microdollars: `closed_slice`, `probe` — a few cents a day, for a run where the quota runs out, — and `dev_check`, for the account of `dev-check` on the dev projects alone: `invite` and `tariff` refuse it run as a service or a job, and `serve` deployed reads it as a tariff with no caps. There too are the typical tasks, `quota.TypicalHandwriting` and `quota.TypicalRubric`, and the reserve by the kind of call, when `tutor` gives no expected price. Stage 3 adds tariffs and payment, and the rule of the quota does not change (Д10 in [DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)).

**Exhausted** — the profile's or the account's cap does not cover the request's reserve. Then there is no call, but this does not reject the submission or the words: in the protocol it is an outcome in a successful answer, and a refusal only for "explain differently", at the start of an attempt and of a segment:

| Call | What the learner gets |
|---|---|
| reading handwriting and a choice written by hand | "unchecked" and a neutral phrase; the rung does not grow |
| the words of a rung | the fallback text of the rung |
| "explain differently" | there is no call, and the right to it stays |
| the correct answer words | there are no words: the verdict and the solution come without them |
| an essay | there is no reading: the submission ends "unchecked", the learner sees a neutral phrase, and the attempt waits. They can press "Done" then too |
| a tapped choice | is checked as always, without AI |

The outcome, and for "explain differently" the refusal, carries the cause "quota" and the time when the next family day begins. The text is in `app`: it names no numbers, but it may promise "tomorrow".

**In the middle of a task:**
- the attempt waits. It is open until a new attempt or the limit of 7 days closes it, and `app` says that checking will come back tomorrow. Tomorrow the learner continues the same attempt, if they have not left it;
- a new task with handwriting `api` begins only if the remainder covers a typical task — two checks, words and praise; this is a value of the configuration. A task with a rubric has its own — two readings of the essay with an assessment, the first submission and one rewrite. Otherwise the learner would begin work that nobody can read today;
- while the attempt waits, `api` does not offer a new task by itself: the engine is called only when the learner asks for another task. If they asked, the waiting attempt is closed with the reason "left", as always, the wrong answers before leaving give "left unfinished", and `app` warns that there is no coming back to it. A new close reason or "unchecked" would hide the wrong answers from the learner model;
- a lesson's segment waits the same way: the lesson run is open, and the waiting is not recorded separately. If the learner asked for another task, "left" closes only the attempt, and the next day the lesson goes on from the same segment.

**AI closed for a new task.** The rule is one for three causes — a quota that does not cover a typical task, the admission refusal "not admitted" and the provider's refusal by a limit: `api` gives the engine only tasks that are checked without AI, as `tutor` requires for admission. In the first slice there are few such tasks — the work is written by hand almost everywhere, — and then the learner sees that there is no checking until tomorrow, and on the provider's refusal, that there is none for now. The pool of tasks and the lessons are set for the engine by `api`: when AI is closed, the pool holds only tasks that are checked without AI — each of their answer slots is chosen by a tap, — the lessons are then only of such tasks, and the pool may be empty. The engine does not know the cause, and the choice stores whether the pool was so limited. A lesson run already begun waits meanwhile, and what the engine gets about lessons is set by its card in `spec`.

**The provider's refusal by a limit.** On the platform's key this is the outcome "limit reached" of `tutor`: a limit of the organization or the workspace, the provider's tier limit, the balance or the payment. Such a limit at the provider is monthly, and the refusal does not promise "tomorrow": its cause is "platform limit". `api` closes platform AI for everyone — by a flag in the database, one per deployment, — until an operator's command opens it again. The event code `api` writes into the log without an ID: it is a signal to the operator. The spending limit of the workspace at the provider is set by the author.

In the code: a new task with handwriting on platform AI — by the engine choice, "again" and as a lesson's segment — begins only if platform AI is open and the day that the account holds has enough left for a typical task, `quota.TypicalHandwriting`, for the profile and for the account; otherwise the refusal `resource_exhausted` with `CAUSE_QUOTA` and `next_family_day` or `CAUSE_PLATFORM_LIMIT`, and for an account without a tariff with caps, `CAUSE_NOT_ADMITTED`. An open attempt of a segment is given as it is and waits. The engine choice asks the same: AI closed by the quota or by the flag leaves only tapped tasks in the pool and the lessons, the record of the choice marks the pool as limited, and "nothing to give" carries the outcome "quota" with the time of the next day, "platform limit" or "not admitted" — the migration `0016` stores the cause and the time for a repeat. The flag — the row `platform_ai` — is read on each request, not cached: any instance sets it, and only the operator clears it. It is set by the outcome "limit reached" of a call on the platform's key, right after the call, and the first such setting is the warning `platform_limit` in the log, without an ID. While the flag is set, neither reading, nor words, nor "differently" on platform AI call the provider: "unchecked" and the fallback text with the outcome "platform limit", and for "differently", a refusal. Platform AI is opened again by the command `api reopen`. An own key is touched by neither the quota nor the flag. Every engine choice goes by the route that its request names — `own_key_provider`, or at `SubmitWork` the provider of `own_key` —, as the start by the same route would: on the own key the pool and the runs are judged by the admission of its provider alone, with no quota and no flag, and "nothing to give" carries "not admitted" alone; a profile not of the category `adult` that names a provider is refused (Д224 spec). An open lesson run the engine gives by its first branch, even when the lesson is not among those that `api` gave it, so an open run whose next task segment the code will not begin now — AI is closed by the quota, the flag or admission — does not go to the engine, nor does its lesson. The learner gets a tapped task or "nothing to give" with an outcome, and the run stays open and waits: once AI opens, the engine gives it again.

**The adult** sees in their section the share of today's quota of the profile and the account and when the next day begins, not dollars, and the learner the share of their own profile alone, where they begin work. The view is `app`'s; the request is `AccountService.GetQuota` of the contract, and the tariff comes with what is decided of tariffs (О-14). In the code: the share is the settled spending of the counters of the day the account keeps, read by `store.QuotaSettled` without a write and without the reserves, as `quota.Share` — whole percent rounded down, 100 at the cap and past it; `cap_reached` is either cap reached, and a tariff without caps gives no share; `next_family_day` is the end of that day.

**Reserve and settlement:**
1. Before each request that calls platform AI, `api` takes a reserve on the counters of the profile and the account in one transaction. The request goes if on each of them what is spent, what is in reserve and the new reserve together are no more than the cap. So two devices of one account do not slip past the cap at once.
2. The reserve is the expected price of the kind of call. `tutor` gives it by its price table and the model of the kind, and until then, a value by kind in the configuration of `api`. The worst case does not do: in `tutor` it is 4,000 tokens of input and 16,000 of output ([be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs)), for Opus 5.5 about $0.34 by the prices of sources 7.1, and the profile's cap would run out on the second reserve.
3. A check of handwriting reserves both readers, or the one of `single`, an essay the reading with the assessment, a workbook page by its crops, a task from paper its Check whole, the cascade of its models. So the quota does not run out mid-check.
4. Settlement is in the day when the reserve was taken:
   - an exact spending record is counted at its own price;
   - a record by the worst case — for an answer cut off before the tokens were counted — is counted as the reserve: a failure of the platform does not eat a child's day. The worst case goes into the spending log;
   - there was no call — the reserve is returned;
   - the provider refused one reader by a limit — only the record of the one that answered is counted;
   - there is no spending message at all — the reserve stays;
   - the process crashed — the reserve stays as the spending of its day, as in canvas-api a call that was made was counted even on a failure ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs)).
5. Overspending beyond the cap is no more than the difference between the exact price and the reserve for each running request. It is held by the limited `MaxOutputTokens` of each kind of product call, and for the words whose instructions the cache keeps, by the premium of a write to the cache over the input on the instructions, which the expected price does not count.

In the code: the reserve is taken by the first transaction of the request — the same one that writes the family day, — by `Tutor.Price` of the kind of call through the admission gate, and without an expected price, by the value of the kind from `internal/quota`. Reading a sheet is reserved by `CallSheet`, the words of a rung with the work by `CallWords`, and those of a task a tap checks and "explain differently" by `CallHint`, which `tutor` gives no expected price of, so by its value in `internal/quota`. The correct answer words reserve by `CallCorrect`, whose expected price `tutor` gives, so `internal/quota` has no value of it. If it did not fit, there is no call: for reading, "unchecked" and the outcome "quota" with the time the next day begins, for the words of a rung, the fallback text with the same outcome, for "explain differently", the refusal `resource_exhausted` with `next_family_day`, and the day written by the request stays. A tariff without caps is the outcome or the refusal "not admitted", without the next day. Settlement is right after the call, in a transaction of its own on a context that the end of the request does not cut off, before the second transaction: the call has already been made, and whatever happens to the answer, it is counted. The price is by the settlement table above: exact records by their sum, a record by the worst case and a call without records as the reserve, "not admitted" as zero. The row `ai_usage` writes the price of each model `cost_micro_usd` and the sign `billed`, its tokens of the cache `cache_write_tokens` and `cache_read_tokens` beside `input_tokens` without them, and its own time `model_ms`, its `retries` and `first_token_ms`, and an exhaustion is a row of its own `quota_exhausted` with the cap that the request hit: `profile`, `account` or `tariff`. Stopping an instance needs no settlement mechanism of its own: the requests that the server managed to wait for settle themselves, and one cut off by the stop leaves the reserve as the spending of its day, like a crashed process.

**The estimate of spending** — for the size of the caps and for О-14. The prices of calls are from the measurements of `tutor`, by Anthropic's prices, checked 2026-11-02 (sources 7.1; [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs)). This is a guideline, not a measurement of the product:

| the words of a rung — the analysis and the hint, Opus 5.5 · `high` | ≈ $0.0152 | [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/be607e5/docs/architecture.md) |

- The profile's cap of `closed_slice` in `internal/quota` is sized by this estimate, about 32 tasks in any mode, and О-14 holds the same estimate.
- At an account's cap of $3.00 a day, the size of `closed_slice`, an account's worst month is 30 × $3.00 = $90. With days no shorter than 20 hours there are no more than 36 days in 30 calendar days, so the worst month is no more than $108, plus overspending. The budget of the closed slice is the number of accounts × $108, and from above it is held by the workspace's limit at the provider.
- Stage 3 does not inherit these caps. If Plus for $10 a month with a quota five times the free one pays for the worst month, a free day is no more than $10 / (5 × 36) ≈ $0.056 at 36 days in a month, less than two tasks in the mode "self" at ≈ $0.031 per task. So checking must get cheaper by about an order of magnitude, or the prices will be different: this is an input for О-14.

**What is kept and for how long** — the rows "account" and "quota counters" of the table of the data `api` stores:
- the counters of the profile and the account — the start of the family day, spent and in reserve. The key is the start of the day, not the date: on a change of the offset the family's date may repeat;
- in the account — the tariff, the offset and the end of the current day, in the invite code — the tariff, and the flag "platform AI closed" — one per deployment, in the database of `api`. The code's tariff and the flag carry no data of the learner or the owner, and they are not in that table: codes are not personal data;
- a counter is needed for its own day and one more day after its end — for settling requests across midnight, — and then the operator's daily job of retention periods deletes it. A counter lives no longer than 2 days after the end of its day, and leaves the history of Neon one day later. It is created with the first reserve of the day, so a day without requests has no counter. П-9 holds this term;
- into the export go the tariff, the offset, the end of the day and all stored counters: everything but secrets and service fields.

In the code: the migration `0015` — the tables `account_quota` and `profile_quota`, a row per day with a reserve, with the key "owner and start of the day" and the end of the day, by which the step `quota` of the job `api sweep` deletes them when the day ended more than a day ago, — and the table `platform_ai` of one row of the flag. The reserve is `store.ReserveQuota`: in the transaction that already holds the account's row by the idempotency key, it reads the day's counters `FOR UPDATE`, checks both caps of the tariff and adds to the reserve of both. So two reserves of one account go one after another. `store.QuotaFits` is the same check without a write, for a typical task. Settlement is `store.SettleQuota`, in a transaction of its own: the reserve goes, and the price of the call is added to what is spent. Deleting a profile takes its counters with it by the key `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and deleting an account, the account's counters too. In the export — `QuotaCounters` of the profile and the account.

**Deletion:**
- deleting a profile takes its counters, and the account's counters stay: otherwise a deletion and a new profile would lift the spending cap ([canvas-api at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs)). The order of deletion is in the design of deletion and export;
- deleting an account takes everything. A new account needs a new one-time code, so the cap is not lifted then either. With the open sign-in of stage 3 this stops holding — Д10.

**Logs and unit economics** — without IDs:
- into the log go the spending message of `tutor`, which has neither content nor identifiers, and the event codes "quota exhausted" and "platform limit";
- unit economics reads the sums by kind of call and by model and the number of exhaustions per day. Summaries by account `api` does not write: with a handful of participants of the closed slice they are personal data.

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- the counters and the offset are pseudonymous data about when and how much the family learns. They do not go to AI, and the logs have neither IDs nor the offset. What goes to AI in each call the quota does not change;
- the offset is a coarse location, and the privacy document of `spec` promises, by the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, that there is no geolocation. The clock offset does not fall under this promise: it names a time zone, not the street and city that COPPA §312.2 calls geolocation (sources 3.1 spec), — it is data of the account, which the policy speaks of (П-23 closed);
- the privacy document of `spec` names the tariff, the clock offset and the end of the day in the account, and the fields of the account of `api` and the quota counters by a reference to this document;
- the period of the counters is no longer than 2 days after the end of their day, as П-9 holds.

**What is needed when:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| the first app — the author, locally | the author's calls go by the platform AI route, with a key from an environment variable on their machine, and the quota counts them by the tariff of their account |
| platform AI opens to anyone but the author — the first deployment with the DPA fact declared | the quota in the code, checked in a run with `app` (measurements 3), and the spending limit of the workspace at the provider, which the author sets |
| stage 3 | tariffs beyond the "closed slice", payment and subscription, the cap with open sign-in (Д10) |

**What the neighbours hold:**
- О-14 and П-9 hold the rule of the quota of stage 2, the estimate of spending and the period of the counters. A free quota for children and grants are the question О-5;
- `tutor` — the worst price of each kind of call and the expected price of the kinds it has measured, the limited `MaxOutputTokens` of each kind of product call and the cause of the outcome "limit reached";
- `engine` — a pool that may be limited and empty, and what the engine gives on an empty one. What the engine gets about lessons and when it is called is set by its card in `spec`, and the design is that of the lesson, below.

**In the protocol:** the clock offset comes with requests to platform AI, and with an own key there is none. "Quota", "platform limit" and "not admitted" are one enumeration of causes: at the beginning of an attempt and of a segment, and for "explain differently" without a reserve, it is a refusal, and for a submission and words, an outcome in a successful answer, for "quota" with the time of the next day. "Explain differently" goes without the work, and the own key and its provider come with every request of the owner profile that calls AI, with "differently" too. A provider's refusal by a limit is the outcome "limit reached" with a cause, a refusal of the key itself is the outcome "key refused", and neither moves the call to platform AI. The share of the quota is `GetQuota`, which carries no offset: its day is the one the account keeps (above, «The adult»).

### 3.7. Lesson

What the neighbours decided about the lesson is in [`app` at 3ce4cdb](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/tree/3ce4cdb/docs), [`learner-model` at 20ae268](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/20ae268/docs/architecture.md), [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs), [`engine` at 08bc48a](https://github.com/altnovatrail/engine/tree/08bc48a), [`engine` at 9751ba4](https://github.com/altnovatrail/engine/blob/9751ba4/docs/decisions.md) and [`skill-map` at 0295ce8](https://github.com/altnovatrail/skill-map/blob/0295ce8/docs/architecture.md).

**What the decision starts from:**
- a lesson is a module's file `lessons/<id>.yaml`: the module's skills that it teaches, and segments in the author's order — an explanation, a worked example and a task of the module by its ID. There is no special check segment at the end: the check is the lesson's last tasks. A module may have no lessons, there is a lesson without tasks — `reading-model-essays.yaml` —, and an explanation may come after the last task — `gaps-and-posts.yaml` and `first-sentences.yaml` (`spec/format/examples/lesson/valid/`);
- a task segment ends when the task has ended, "as the server decides by the hint ladder and the solution shown", and only then does the next one begin. The engine does not reorder segments;
- the open view of a lesson — without skills and age categories — goes to the app whole, and the device part, one for all learners, has no open views of lessons: `api` gives a lesson only to a learner whom both the lesson and each of its tasks suit. "Otherwise a task only for adults (П-20) would reach a child in a lesson where nothing is skipped". A lesson that no learner will get is an error of the module, and the leak check searches for the answer of each task of the lesson in all its texts;
- a lesson names a template as it names a task: the learner model and the engine see one task, and the attempt's seed chooses the instance. A repeat of a template is a new attempt seed;
- a lesson holds the digest of the package it began on, all its tasks go on this package, and a withdrawn version or a version outside the window ends the lesson "unchecked". The skills of a lesson on an earlier package go to the engine mapped by `retired_skills` of the current version;
- the order of lessons is set by the skill graph, and where it gives no order, the engine's rule chooses. When the engine gives a lesson and when a task outside a lesson is decided in `engine`, and the first slice has no consolidation within a lesson and no early end of a lesson. At the engine's input are "the lessons and the pool of tasks: the lesson's skills and its segments";
- the lesson player is `app`'s. `app` keeps the state of the demo of a worked example itself, and after a break it returns the learner to where they were. `app`'s side ([3ce4cdb](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/tree/3ce4cdb/docs)): the place in the lesson and the state of the demo are its own, by the key "lesson ID, package digest"; leaving mid-lesson sends nothing, and when `api` gives the lesson again on the same package, `app` opens it at that place; `app` sends "lesson passed" itself, on "Continue" at the last segment. Whether a lesson begun on another device continues the same run, and what is recorded about a passed lesson, are `api`'s, below;
- the age category is confirmed once a year, and when the country changes, the age is given again;
- a worked example is neither a task nor evidence, and a lesson adds no call of `tutor` ([`learner-model` at 20ae268](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/20ae268/docs/architecture.md); [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs));
- a lesson's task is the same attempt as outside a lesson, and a profile has one open attempt.

**Lesson run** — a row of `api`'s database per profile and lesson, one for all devices. The term is the same as `app`'s:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| run ID | the answers about the lesson, the segment's attempt and "lesson passed" name it — so `app` tells apart the runs on one package |
| profile ID, the lesson's full ID, the module ID and its version | whose lesson and which; the version — so that the export reads without the index, as for an attempt |
| package digest | all the lesson's tasks go on it |
| the engine choice that gave the lesson | an adult sees the reason code |
| time of the beginning and of the last action | the term "abandoned" and the repeat of the engine choice |
| end reason and time | below; empty for an open one |

- The beginning, the end reason and the end time, once recorded, do not change.
- The attempt of a lesson's task carries the run ID and the segment place, from 1: a segment has no ID, its place names it. The first slice knows no consolidation attempt within a lesson; if a new version of the engine introduces one, its place is "after segment k".
- An open run is one per profile and lesson, and a database constraint holds this. A second simultaneous request — from another device — rereads the row and gives the open one. In code the constraint is a unique partial index `lesson_runs (profile_id, lesson_id) WHERE ended_at IS NULL`, and an abandoned run is ended by the step `abandoned_runs` of the job `api sweep`; the second insert gets `store.ErrRunBegun`, in a savepoint of its own, and the request rereads the open run. There may be several open runs of different lessons.
- An open run whose next task segment — the first task segment that has not ended — the code will not begin now does not go to the engine, and neither does its lesson: it waits until the segment begins again. A run with a segment that the code will not begin at all — a task with a rubric or on a workbook at the rung `full`, which admits none of their kinds of call — waits the same way (Д26 in [DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)). A run all of whose task segments have ended waits only for "lesson passed" and always goes to the engine, and a run on a package that the image no longer gives does not wait: if open, it ends "unchecked".

**How `app` gets a lesson:**
1. The engine has chosen a lesson. The choice itself begins nothing.
2. `app` asks for the open view of the lesson that the choice named. If the lesson has an open run, `api` gives it. If not, it begins a new one on the package of the current version, after the full check (below).
3. The answer is the run ID, the lesson's open view whole, the package digest, the places of the task segments that have ended, and the segment's open attempt, if there is one.
4. `api` gives an open run again by its ID — after a restart and on another device —, again after the full check. `app` gets the profile's open runs and its open attempt with the profile: so a device that has no state of the lesson finds them too.

There is no other way to a new run: the engine chooses a lesson or a task, and `app` has no list of lessons. A request for a lesson without an open run does not begin a new one: the refusal names how the last one ended. A lesson that does not suit a new run, or that the current version does not have, is a refusal without the open view, and the engine does not get such lessons at all. In the protocol these are the refusals "run ended" — with how it ended —, "not suitable" and "no current version".

**Full check** — every time `api` gives a lesson and begins a segment's attempt:
- the module is connected, it has a current version, and that version suits the profile by audience;
- the lesson and each of its tasks suit the profile's age category: for a new run, by the package of the current version, for an open one, by the run's package, the one the learner sees;
- if it fails for an open run, the run ends "not suitable", and its open attempt is closed with the reason "not suitable", with evidence, as for "left".

Open attempts outside a lesson are not checked again: a task is checked at the beginning of the attempt, and such an attempt brings no new tasks. "Lesson passed" does not serve as a check for the same reason: it brings nothing to the device, and the run records what was.

**Task segment:**
- a segment's attempt begins only if the run is open, every task segment before it has ended, and the segment itself has not. An open attempt of the segment is given back the same;
- it is an attempt on the run's package, with its own seed, the same as outside a lesson. A new one closes another open one with the reason "left", and a new attempt of the same segment is a new seed, and for a template perhaps another instance;
- the quota and closed AI concern only a task that needs platform AI; a tapped choice and an own key do not spend the quota. If what is left is not enough for a typical task, the refusal "quota" with the time of the next day; if AI is closed, a refusal with a cause. The run stays open;
- a segment has ended when its attempt is closed "correct" or "solution shown", and for a task with a rubric "done":

| Segment's attempt | Segment | Run |
|---|---|---|
| closed "correct" or "solution shown", and for a task with a rubric "done" | ended | `api` answers what next: the place of the next segment or "no more segments" |
| submission verdict "unchecked" — the quota, an AI failure | not ended: the attempt waits | open |
| closed "left" or "abandoned" | not ended: on return, a new attempt | open |
| closed "unchecked" — the version is withdrawn or outside the window | — | ends "unchecked" in the same transaction |
| closed "not suitable": the full check failed | — | ends "not suitable" in the same transaction |

The segment of a task with a rubric ends with its attempt: such a task has neither the ladder nor showing the solution, and the learner's "Done" closes the attempt — with the reason "done".

"Unchecked" stands in four places with one meaning — there is no check, and it is not the learner's error: the submission verdict "unchecked" does not close the attempt, nor does a submission with a rubric that ended "unchecked", but that is not a verdict: an item with a rubric has none. The close reason "unchecked" closes the attempt, and the run end reason "unchecked" ends the lesson. So the word always comes with what it is about.

**Run end** — the run end reasons:

| Reason | When |
|---|---|
| "passed" | `app` sent "lesson passed" with the run ID, and all task segments have ended. Before that, a refusal with the places of the segments that have ended. If the run has already ended, the answer names the reason, and for "passed" this is not an error: "lesson passed" may come twice too. The package check on request goes first, so on a withdrawn version the run ends "unchecked" |
| "unchecked" | a lesson's attempt was closed with this reason, or the job after a deployment and the check on request found a run on a package that the image no longer gives |
| "not suitable" | the full check failed |
| "abandoned" | 30 days without actions. An action is any request about the run and its attempts: the lesson's open view, a segment's attempt and its requests, "lesson passed"; closing by a job does not count as an action. `app` opens the lesson by `api`'s answer, so the run lives while the learner returns to the lesson. The check on request and the operator's daily retention job close it, and the closing does not move the profile's activity date |
| "early" | a place for an early end of a lesson by the engine. The engine's rule has none, and the contract has no such reason: it comes as a new reason if the rule introduces it. `api` does not end a lesson early, and it is not among the end reasons in the database or in code |

If the learner left mid-lesson, the run is open, and `app` sends nothing. If "lesson passed" did not come — for example, the learner closed the app after the last task —, the run is open, and the next "Continue" ends it. If the module was disconnected with an open run, the run ends by the general rules, and the engine does not give it.

**Engine:**
- if a run ended by a request, `api` calls the engine for the next one, and if it ended by a job, the engine is called when the learner asks;
- the engine is not called at the end of a segment: segments go in the author's order, and the first slice has no consolidation and no early end, by the engine's rule. If a new version of the engine introduces them, `api` will call it at the end of a segment of a lesson whose skills are all assessed by verdict, and the answer is the next segment, consolidation or "early";
- the skill chosen on the skill map holds: `api` calls the engine with it at the end of a run too, whatever code the choice that gave the lesson came with, `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` too;
- at the engine's input are the lessons of the current versions of the connected modules that suit the profile fully, and when AI is closed, without the lessons whose tasks need AI, and the profile's runs: the lesson, its skills, the end reason — an open run has none — and the end time. The engine's rule does not read the places of ended segments, and `api` does not pass them. The skills of the lesson of an open run on an earlier package are from that package, mapped by `retired_skills` of the current version;
- the choice stores its own time too: by it the repeat of a choice restores which runs the engine saw.
- in code a run that the request itself ended — "lesson passed" or a check that found the end — carries the engine choice in `RunEnd`, with the skill that the last choice holds, on the route to AI that the request names — `own_key_provider` of "open a lesson", "open a run" and "lesson passed", and of the start of a segment; a repeat of "lesson passed" gets the same choice. Lessons whose task segment the code will not begin by that route do not go to the engine (Д26).

What `engine` needs for this, `spec` holds: within a lesson the engine is not called, and consolidation and "early" are a place for its detailing, which the first slice does not have; it does not read the places of ended segments. The line of `api`'s card in `spec` "by the result of an attempt, evidence into `learner-model`, then `engine` chooses the next lesson or task" means the next segment within a lesson.

**Module version:** an open run goes on its package to the end, whatever version becomes current. A lesson whose run has ended is, by a new choice, a new run on the current version, from the first segment. The package of an open run is in the image: its format version is in the window, and otherwise the run has ended "unchecked".

**Quota and AI:** beginning a run needs no AI — explanations and examples are read without it. If the quota ran out mid-lesson, the waiting is not recorded separately: the run is open, and the segment's attempt waits or has not begun. A request for another task closes only the attempt — "left" —, and the next day the lesson continues from the same segment.

**What `api` does not know:** the place among the explanations and examples, and the state of the demo of a worked example. `app` holds them, a worked example is not evidence, and data are kept to a minimum. On another device the lesson opens after the last segment that has ended, and the demos from the start.

**Evidence:** a run has none of its own. A segment's attempt gives evidence, as outside a lesson, and a worked example gives none. Whether the weight of evidence needs the fact "in a lesson" — the attempt knows its run — is for `learner-model` to decide: its card in `spec` names this point.

**`skill-map`** does not draw lessons and asks nothing about them ([`skill-map` at 0295ce8](https://github.com/altnovatrail/skill-map/blob/0295ce8/docs/architecture.md)). `app` gets the tiers for the label "sequential" with a connected module — `skill_tiers` of the current version, even before the first attempt.

**What is stored and for how long** — the rows "lesson runs" and "attempt" of the table of what the database stores, above: a run lives while the profile lives (П-9), and an open one without actions for 30 days ends "abandoned"; all the fields go into the export, and a run is deleted with the profile.

**In code** (`internal/learning`, `lesson.go`):
- the run check goes at opening, by ID and before a segment's attempt: a package that the index does not know is a refusal without a record; a withdrawn package, "unchecked"; 30 days without actions, "abandoned"; the module, the current version, the lesson and its tasks, "not suitable". The end is recorded, and the request gets "run ended" with the reason; "lesson passed" checks only the package and the term;
- a run's last action is the later of what is recorded in its row and the last action of its attempts; the job `api sweep` counts it the same way. A request on a lesson's attempt first locks its run, as the requests on a run do, so closing an attempt and ending a run take the rows in one order;
- a segment's attempt abandoned by the term is closed when the segment begins, and the same request begins a new one; an open run of the lesson from another choice that the check has ended does not stop the opening: the new choice begins its own;
- the code reads the places of ended segments from the run's attempts, and the run of a segment's attempt closed "unchecked" is ended by a database trigger;
- `next` is the place of the next segment of any kind or "no more segments", in the answers that close an attempt — a correct answer, the solution shown and "Done" —, and a repeat of "Done" gives it again by the state of the attempt;
- the segment of a task with a rubric ends once its attempt is closed "done", and its run is passed once every task segment has ended;
- a segment that the code will not begin — a task with a rubric or handwriting without admission of its kinds of call, a sheet's, a workbook page's or an essay's with its rubric's — is not checked when the lesson opens: the segment's attempt is "not admitted", and the run stays open (Д26 in [DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)).

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- a run is pseudonymous data about what the learner studied and when, without content: no texts of the lesson, no place among the explanations, no state of the demo. Everything in it goes into the export;
- nothing of a run goes to AI, and `api` writes neither the ID nor the lesson into its logs;
- the learner does not write on the sheet of a worked example: there is no handwriting there, and the promise that handwriting stays on the device is not touched;
- the term is as for attempts (П-9), and the 30 days only end an open run and do not delete the row;
- the privacy document of `spec` names lesson runs in a row of their own, "Lesson runs", and П-9 their term; `api`'s register of fields is the table of what the database stores.

In the protocol — opening a lesson by the engine choice and a run by its ID, a segment's attempt as a variant of beginning an attempt, "lesson passed" and the refusals "run ended", "segments not ended" and "segment unavailable" with the places of ended segments. The answer by which a run ended carries the next engine choice, and the answer to a segment's end, the place of the next segment or "no more segments". The end of an attempt with a rubric, and with it the end of its segment in a lesson, is "Done".
### 3.8. Task with a rubric

What the neighbours decided about the essay is in [`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs), [`app` at 14f5a54](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/tree/14f5a54/docs), [`learner-model` at 28433fa](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/28433fa/docs/architecture.md), [`engine` at 08bc48a](https://github.com/altnovatrail/engine/tree/08bc48a) and [the prototype in `spec` v0.6.2](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.6.2/draft).

**What the decision starts from:**
- in the first slice a task with a rubric is given by the module of short essays in English for any age: an essay by hand on a sheet gets feedback by a rubric — an assessment by the author's criteria —, not a verdict. Tasks with a key in the same module get a verdict. The check `rubric` is only on the whole sheet, and such a task has one item and one answer slot;
- the path is two calls: reading the essay by one reader, without the rubric — the text as written —, then assessing the read text by the criteria of the rubric, without the picture, the key and the task's solution. The model gives for each criterion a justification and whether it is met, and one feedback on the whole essay. Between the calls the text lives in the memory of `api`'s request;
- a criterion has no ID — only a text, and `tutor` numbers the criteria in order. A criterion that is absent from the model's answer or that is named twice `tutor` gives without an assessment, and «Повторять ли тогда вызов, решает `api`»; a failure of the call leaves all the criteria without an assessment. Reading gives «текст, как написан, и можно ли его прочитать», but the measurement prompt did not measure the output "unreadable": the synthetic samples are all legible ([`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs));
- feedback passes the leak check and the feedback rule — no more than three sentences and 80 words in English, and a stretch in quotation marks of four words or more is only the learner's words —, by the code of `tutor`. For a rubric, leaks are searched for only by the task's leak phrases, and the function of the feedback rule answers "passed" or names the broken parts — sentences, words, quote, letters. What the learner sees instead of feedback that did not pass the rule, `api` decides. The format gives a rubric no fallback texts: they belong to the rungs of the ladder, after a wrong value;
- an essay that cannot be read does not reach the assessment, and the learner sees a request to rewrite the sheet, as for "unreadable". But an item with a rubric has no verdict, and "unreadable" is a verdict, so how `api` shows and records this is for it to decide;
- the check of a rubric is closed whole, with the criteria, "until the task is solved", and a solution is optional for a task whose answer slot is only `sheet`. The learner sees feedback "as feedback, not as a grade", and "what to develop" is derived from the learner model;
- what the server stores of an attempt with rubric feedback is П-19, closed: only the AI's assessment by each of the author's criteria, without the essay's text and without the words of the feedback: "they live in the request's memory and on the device", and the text only as an artifact by the account owner's choice. What is read of an essay is its whole text, and the platform promises: "What the learner writes by hand stays on the device";
- on the recommendation of О-17, rubric feedback does not go into the learner model in the first slice. There is no need to repeat feedback from the observation: an observation repeats a verdict, and rubric feedback has none (П-19);
- assessment by a rubric is measured by false alarms on met criteria and by agreement with people's assessment (О-22 closed) — this is the eval of `tutor`, not of `api`;
- what a module may ask a learner under 18 to write about is П-20: a module may give a task about the learner's life only to adults, through the task's `age_categories`, and `api` gives a task only to learners of its categories;
- `app` keeps the essay with what was read and the feedback on the device as an artifact, until an adult erases it. «Что идёт после отзыва, решает `api`», and "Continue" on a lesson's task appears only when `api` has said that the task has ended ([`app` at 14f5a54](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/tree/14f5a54/docs));
- in the prototype the essay "goes Plan, Write, Improve and ends with "I'm done"", and that "nothing checks" it, `spec` replaced with feedback by a rubric (`spec/draft/README.md`).

**Path** — one request of `app`, two calls of `tutor`:
1. `app` sends the crop of the sheet. `api` refuses a submission to a closed attempt before calling AI.
2. `api` takes a reserve for reading together with the assessment. If it is not enough, there are no calls, and the submission ends "unchecked".
3. Reading the essay by one reader — with the work, the task's open view, the age category and the AI route. If the sheet as a whole cannot be read, the submission ends "unreadable".
4. Assessment by the rubric — with the read text, the task statement as the topic, the criteria in order, the presentation style, the explanation language, the age category and the AI route.
5. Feedback passes the leak check and the feedback rule, by the code of `tutor`.
6. One transaction records the submission: the time, how it ended, and for feedback the criteria's marks and the source of the words. The spending is counted against the quota, as for any check.
7. The answer is the read text and the words of the feedback, or a note that there are none.

**How a submission ends.** This is not a verdict: an item with a rubric has none.

| Submission end | When | What the learner sees | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| feedback | the assessment returned at least one criterion with an assessment | the read text and the words of the feedback, and if they did not pass the leak check or the feedback rule, the phrase of `app` "no feedback this time"; the call is not repeated | the time, the mark of each criterion and the source of the words — the model or the phrase "no feedback this time" |
| "unreadable" | reading said that the sheet as a whole cannot be read; the assessment is not called, and its reserve is returned | a request to rewrite the sheet — the same text of `app` as for the verdict "unreadable"; there is no text | the time |
| "unchecked" | an AI failure, an exhausted quota, "not admitted", a provider's refusal by a limit, or an assessment that returned no criterion with an assessment | a neutral phrase, and for the quota also the time of the next day; the read text, if there was reading | the time |

- A criterion's mark is "met", "not met" or "not assessed": these are the platform's words for a mark, and "not yet" is a value of a verdict. Not assessed is a criterion that is absent from the model's answer or named twice; `api` does not repeat the call.
- "Unreadable" and "unchecked" mean the same as the verdicts of the same name — it is not the learner's error —, but they stand at a submission. The three ends have no common name: "result" in the glossary already means both feedback and the end of an attempt.
- "Unreadable" is only for the sheet as a whole: a single illegible word must not send the whole essay to rewriting. How reading does this is the question Т-37.
- The marks are recorded even when the words of the feedback did not pass the checks: the assessment was made, and only the words did not pass. The phrase "no feedback this time" is not the neutral phrase: that one says there is no check, and here there was an assessment.

**What goes to the device:** the read text, if there was reading, and the words of the feedback or a note that there are none. The justifications, the marks and the criteria do not go: the learner sees feedback, not an assessment, and the criteria are closed. Without the text, `app` can read again only how the attempt's submissions ended, and their time.

**Rewriting and "Done":**
- the attempt is open for rewriting: the learner corrects the same sheet and sends it again, and each submission is a new reserve and two calls again. The assessment gets neither the earlier feedback nor the earlier text: they are not on the server. A new submission after a lost answer is a new submission like any other, and a repeat of the request with the same idempotency key does not call AI again: `api` answers with what it recorded — how the submission ended —, without what was read and the words of the feedback, so a network failure does not spend the quota twice;
- "Done" is a learning action, `can_learn`. `api` accepts it after at least one submission of the attempt, however that ended, and before that refuses it with the cause "no submission". The attempt is closed with the reason "done" in one transaction, without evidence (О-17). It can also be ended with an empty sheet that gave "unreadable" — an adult sees this in the attempt's submissions;
- "Done" after "unreadable" or "unchecked" is the way out for a learner whose essay cannot be read and for one whose quota has run out: a rubric has no showing the solution, by which the segment of a task with a key ends;
- "left", "abandoned", "unchecked" on a withdrawn version or outside the window, and "not suitable" close an attempt with a rubric like any other, and none of them gives evidence.

**Two devices:** `api` refuses a submission to a closed attempt before calling AI. If the attempt was closed while its submission was going — by "Done" from another device —, how the submission ended is not recorded: the answer carries the feedback from the request's memory and says that the attempt is closed, and the quota is counted as for any submission. In the code the attempt is held again after the calls, and a closed one records nothing.

**Lesson:**
- the segment of a task with a rubric ends when its attempt is closed "done". "Left" and "abandoned" do not end it, as for any segment, and on return the segment begins a new attempt. The essay of a task that is not a template waits on the device, and it is sent again, while for a template a new attempt is a new seed and perhaps another topic, and the essay is written anew, as on return a task with a key is solved anew;
- the engine gets lessons with a segment of a task with a rubric, and `api` begins their runs;
- after "done" in a lesson `api` answers with the place of the next segment, and outside a lesson the engine chooses the next one, as after any attempt.

**Hints:** a rubric has no ladder, no showing the solution and no "explain differently". "Hint" gives what a task with a key gets before the first wrong answer: the IDs of the task's techniques, empty if there are none, and `app` shows the givens and unknown from the task's open view. There is no refusal "nothing to hint": the empty list is that answer.

**Quota:**
- the reserve is for reading together with the assessment; for "unreadable" the assessment's reserve is returned, and the rest is counted as for any check;
- `api` begins a new task with a rubric only if what is left is enough for a typical task with a rubric — two readings of the essay with an assessment, the first submission and one rewrite, `quota.TypicalRubric`, by the essay price at `tutor` ([be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs));
- if the quota runs out mid-essay, the submission ends "unchecked", and the attempt waits until the next day, like any. The learner can press "Done" then too;
- an own key does not spend the quota.

**What is stored** — the rows "attempt with a rubric", "observation" and "stored works" of the database's table above:
- the attempt's row is like any, without the ladder's fields: a rubric has no wrong answers, rungs or showing the solution;
- each submission has its time and how it ended, and for feedback, the mark of each criterion by its number in the rubric and the source of the words. The number is read by the attempt's package digest;
- the crop of the sheet, the read text, the justifications and the words of the feedback are neither in the database, nor in the repeat queue, nor in the cache of answers or of idempotency, nor in the logs. An essay has no observation row;
- the term is while the profile lives (П-9). Everything listed goes into the export: a criterion by the task ID, the module version and the number, and the package of an official module is open. It is deleted with the profile;
- `api` does not store the essay's text: an essay is stored only as a stored work — its strokes and its picture, as the device holds them —, while whoever consents for the learner keeps the storage of the profile's works on: by default storing is off, turning it off erases the copies on the server in one step, and the works go into the export. In the code this is `WorkService`, the kind "essay" by the task's rubric.

**Who reads the marks:**
- the learner — no: they see feedback, not an assessment;
- an adult — in progress, when the attempt is closed: for one closed "done", the essay is finished, and for one closed otherwise, the rewriting was abandoned. A task with a rubric is solved when its attempt is closed "done", and then its criteria and its solution, if it has one, are open, and "Done" answers with the solved task. Before "done" the criteria and the solution are closed, and the adult sees the marks by the numbers of the criteria; after it the criteria are open, and `app` can name them in words. What stands behind a mark, the adult will read in the feedback that `app` keeps with the essay on the device: the feedback says what to work on, by the criteria;
- the glossary derives "what to develop" from the learner model, and rubric feedback does not go into it (О-17): the marks in progress are the glossary's reservation: for tasks with a rubric, progress reads "what to develop" from the marks;
- the marks do not go to the engine: it gets the closed attempts of tasks with a rubric without marks (О-17).

**Logs** — without IDs and without content: the event code "feedback did not pass the check" — in the code the request's line names the check by its closed set, `empty`, `leak` or `form`, not the part of the rule. By it one sees how often the phrase "no feedback this time" replaces feedback. Neither the text, nor the feedback, nor the justifications, nor marks with IDs are in the logs, as in the logs of `tutor` ([be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/be607e5/docs)).

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- the marks are pseudonymous data, an opinion of AI on the learner's writing. They hold no content of the work, and all of them go into the export;
- the platform's promise "What the learner writes by hand stays on the device" holds: neither the text nor the crop is on the server. Feedback may quote the essay (П-19), so it is not stored either;
- what goes to AI is what the privacy document of `spec` names: for reading, the crop of the sheet; for the assessment, the read text without the picture, and the criteria of the rubric. Nothing of what is stored goes: the assessment does not get the earlier feedback;
- for attempts and observations, the privacy document of `spec` says that what is read of an essay is its whole text, and what is stored is as П-19 was closed;
- П-20: since the server does not store the text, nobody on the platform will see a child's disclosure, in an essay, of harm to themselves or from others. What the platform does with such a disclosure closes П-20: an essay is personal data, the platform does not look for such things, and a learner under 18 is always shown where to turn. The reminder not to write names is `app`'s (П-20).

In the protocol: the answer to a submission with a rubric is the read text, how the submission ended, and the words of the feedback or a note that there are none; "Done" and the refusals "no submission" and "attempt closed"; for "Hint", the IDs of the techniques, empty if there are none; what `app` reads again without the text — how the submissions ended and when — comes with the open attempt. Neither what was read nor the feedback is in the record of the idempotency key either: a repeat of the request with the same key does not call AI again and answers with what is recorded — how the submission ended —, without the text.

**In the code:**
- a task with a rubric is one `tutor` reads as an essay — a handwriting sheet whose one answer slot is bound `sheet` and checked by a rubric —; it begins, enters the pool and the lessons where the gate admits `CallEssay` and `CallRubric` both, and its start asks the quota for `quota.TypicalRubric`. Both are admitted on the models by the author's word, at the rung `author` alone, for the term that `tutor`'s entries of that word name, and "not admitted" at `full` (Д24 in [DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)); the dev mode's stub admits them as the gate does, reads an essay by the shape of its sheet's crop and assesses every criterion met but the last with feedback of its own;
- `SubmitEssay` goes by `rpc.Across`: the attempt held, the crop taken as a sheet's, and the charges of the reading and of the assessment, taken together, each by its value in `internal/quota` without an expected price, beside the record "going"; `ReadEssay` through the port `essayReader`, judged by `observation.Essay` of `module-kit`, and `AssessEssay` through `rubricAssessor` while at least 3 s are left for it, outside transactions; the attempt held again and the submission recorded in the second. A criterion with no mark from `tutor`, which gives one for each in order, is "not assessed";
- the submission is a row of `submissions`, migration `0020`: `essay_end`, `marks` by number and `words_source`, no observation; `answer` and the key's record hold the answer without the text and the feedback. The open attempt carries `essay_submissions`, progress the marks of a closed attempt, the export of version 2 `RubricSubmission`;
- "Done" holds the attempt, refuses a task with a verdict as malformed and an attempt with no submission of any end as "no submission", and otherwise closes it "done" by the path of every close — an attempt with a rubric owes no evidence — and answers with `SolvedTask` — the texts of the criteria in order, no keys, the solution with the format's version — and `next`: in a lesson the next segment or "no more segments", outside a lesson the engine's choice on the route of `own_key_provider`. A repeat on an attempt closed "done" answers the same from the package, the instance and the choice the close gave, with no call of the engine; one on a package the index no longer knows is "package unknown";
- the engine's choice counts an essay's two calls admitted together as AI that reads the work, so a pool where only they are admitted is not limited to the tasks a tap checks, and a task with a rubric left out by its admission alone narrows the pool; on platform AI it asks the quota for room for the larger of `quota.TypicalHandwriting` and `quota.TypicalRubric`. On the real gate a task with a rubric reaches the engine at the rung `author` alone, and on the stub likewise (Д26).

### 3.9. Protocol between `app` and `api`

This section holds the requests, the boundary with the contract of `spec`, refusals and outcomes, idempotency keys and what goes to the device; the wire, the paths without a token, version withdrawal, CORS and logs; no real-time channel in the first slice (Т-25); and where a refusal and where an outcome. The facts about Connect, protobuf, buf and Fetch are in the records sources 10.1 and 10.2, and the libraries' code, the measurements of `tutor` and canvas-api are links beside the claims.

#### What the contract decided and the boundary of `spec` and `api`

**What the contract decided.** The contract `app` ↔ `api` is the package `novatrail.api.v1` of `spec`, at the submodule's tag: its services, one enumeration of causes `Cause`, and the text of the contracts, [`spec/contracts/README.md`](../spec/contracts/README.md). The messages, fields, causes and Connect codes are its own: where the tables below say otherwise, the contract is right. Where it differs from this section or adds to it, it decided:
- the clock offset is only for requests to platform AI, and with an own key there is none; `BeginAttempt` and `ChooseNext` do not call AI and carry no offset;
- the words of a rung and "explain differently" carry the number of wrong answers that the learner saw, and the refusal "answer not current" — `failed_precondition` — comes when the learner asks for words for a wrong answer that is no longer the last: while they were reading, another submission came; for the words of a rung, also the refusals "no rung", "no more words" — from the fourth wrong answer — and "attempt closed", and a repeat comes without marks, with the flag "repeat";
- "version withdrawn" is only for the four open views, and for a work put of an attempt on a version out of the window with no work of it kept before. An attempt on a package that the image no longer gives, `api` closes "unchecked" and answers "attempt closed", and a run, "unchecked" and "run ended";
- the causes "already exists" — `already_exists` — for a second account, a second profile and a second beginning of an attempt by the same choice, and "PIN not set" — `failed_precondition`. No right — `permission_denied` without a cause, a form error — `invalid_argument` without a cause, a failure of the token check — `unavailable` without a cause;
- "waiting for consent" and the PIN requests carry an idempotency key too, and the key's record also catches the same key on another request, not only on another method or target (the idempotency keys of the service as a whole, below);
- the account in the answer has neither the tariff nor the date of the statement, and the PIN check answers how many attempts are left and when the lock ends; "change a forgotten PIN" sets the PIN also when there was none;
- a profile is created by three requests: the owner's own profile — `CreateOwnerProfile`, a child's profile — "waiting for consent" with the age in years and the country, then `CreateChildProfile` by the consent ID, and while the consent is waiting, the refusal "no consent". `api` does not store the age: it computes the category from it. The contract has no change of country;
- the module catalog is by audience, without language; the tiers come with the skill graph, not with the list of connected modules; "no current version" is an empty package digest;
- the engine choice goes to the device without the reason code: the adult sees the code and the time of the choice in progress, at each attempt. Progress has no separate list of choices;
- an adult's progress comes page by page, and the repeating traps are on the first page: a trap repeats when two closed attempts or more found it, not counting attempts on a version withdrawn as `wrong_key`, with `description` and `trap_advice` from the catalog of the last attempt that found it;
- the skill map names the action — the learner's or an adult's —, and by it `api` checks `can_learn` or `can_view`;
- a choice opens one run, and an ended run on the same choice is the refusal "run ended"; "again" is only outside a lesson;
- "Done" answers with the solved task — the criteria and the solution, if there is one; "Hint" on a task with a rubric gives its techniques; a submission of an essay whose attempt was closed while it was going is a field of the answer, not a cause;
- a submission may carry both handwriting and a tapped choice together;
- the own key check has a fifth answer, "not checked", with the outcome "AI failure", and the own key's provider at the beginning of an attempt means that `api` does not check the quota there;
- the path of a file of the device part is `/packages/sha256-<hex>/device/<path>`, where the path is `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` or `media/…`, each segment percent-encoded by RFC 3986;
- the open view is a message with the bytes of the format's JSON and the module format version;
- the export is the methods `ExportProfile` and `ExportAccount`, the bytes of the export format with its version (deletion and export, below). The tariff is not in the contract (О-14).

**The detail of `engine`**, in the same contract:
- `ReasonCode` has the value `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation": a skill already given earlier comes again in rotation, first the one not given for the longest time, though it does not fit the queue `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` — it is mastered, or no skill is reachable. `app` writes the code's words for the adult;
- the skill chosen on the map holds until the learner asks for something else: `api` stores it with the choice and calls the engine with it both for `next` and at the end of a run, and `ChooseNext` without a skill gives the lead back to the engine (the database and learner model, above);
- the run end reasons have no "early": the first slice has no consolidation within a lesson and no early end (the lesson, above).

**The leading question of format 0.4**, in the same contract:
- `GetHintResponse.leading_question` — the leading question of the instance that the attempt got, beside the IDs of the techniques: empty for a task without a question and always empty beside the words of a rung. The server records it as given only when it is not empty (showing the solution and hints, above);
- `OpenAttempt.requested_leading_question` — the same question, given on request, at the open attempt: the format's field is closed, the task's open view does not have it, and the device that continues the attempt shows it from here;
- "what never goes to the device" also includes the leading question beyond what the request gave.

**In the code:**
- `LegalService.GetLegalTexts` without a token gives, on the day of the request in UTC, the current version of every text of the place `app` and its next version, `next_texts`, from `spec.Legal` or from the copy's folder `API_LEGAL_DIR`, read by the rules of the part `legal/`, with `Cache-Control` of an hour and no longer than until 00:00 UTC of the nearest day of a next version it gives; a consent names the current one alone. The folder of a copy is of version 3 of the part, read on the day `serve` or `api check` starts, and `legal.operator` refuses it — no start — without a privacy policy whose day has come, or with a version of it without the section "Who we are";
- the consent methods of `AccountService` and `AttemptService.ReportProblem` carry a key, the review period is 3 days, and the causes 39–42 are theirs; the message limit is 11 MiB, for the scan of the form;
- `WorkService` has its four methods behind the checks of `toLearn`, with the causes 43 and 44, below.

**What the decision starts from:**
- the API contracts are proto3 files in `contracts/` of `spec`, the packages `novatrail.<part>.v1`, the buf rules `STANDARD`, `COMMENTS` and `UNARY_RPC`: no method streams anything. A field's name in JSON is the proto name, snake_case, and `spec` generates the code: the Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec/contracts` and the npm package `@altnovatrail/contracts`. The transport is Connect: the server on connect-go in `api`, the client on connect-es in `app`; JSON or binary protobuf on the wire is decided below;
- the contract's version is the package's suffix and the tag of `spec`. Within a package a change only adds, a breaking change is a new package and a new line, and `api` keeps the current and the previous major package; both sides skip unknown fields. While the module format is v0, `api` and `app` are released together. `app` offers a reload only on `unimplemented` and on a format version that its build does not read. First the contract, then the service, then the client;
- the check of handwriting is two requests of `app`: sending the crop and the verdict, then the tutor's words, for which `app` sends the work again: the server does not store the crop. Reading and analysis are two calls of AI, two readers of one provider read handwriting, and `module-kit` gives the verdict by the observation as a whole. The tutor's words go to the device whole, after the leak check, not as a stream;
- a place in the observation is in thousandths of the crop, 0–1000, and from a workbook the work is a list "item, box, crop", and the place is the answer slot;
- `module-kit` builds the open views by the marks `x-open`; `api` reads them with the Go types `types/formatv0/open`, and `app` with the types of `@altnovatrail/spec`. `app` gets the module manifest, the skill graph, the technique catalog and the solving steps by the package digest, once per package, and caches them, and it deletes the open views of a withdrawn version from the cache. The withdrawal of a version by digest is an answer of its own, distinguishable from a failure, not a conclusion drawn from a refusal to give an open view;
- `app` reads the address of `api` from `/deployment.json`, the part `deployment/` of `spec` — `app` and the deployment of `infra` read it, and `api` does not. `api` stands on the origin of `app` or on its own — `infra` chooses this (Т-17) —, and on its own it answers CORS to the exact origin of `app` on all paths — in the platform's deployment this is `<project>.web.app`, without `firebaseapp.com` —, with `Authorization` among the allowed headers and a preflight term of 7200 s. `app` takes media files by `fetch` and gives the canvas a `Blob`;
- the own key and its provider come with every request of the owner profile that calls AI, with "explain differently" too, and in `api` they live only in the request's memory. `app` names the provider without the key at the beginning of an attempt, and at every request whose answer or refusal may give an engine choice, so that the choice goes by that route; `api` checks the key at connection through `tutor`, and names the providers open to an own key by the admission function;
- the contract carries what `app` needs — the steps and the hint, the mode and voicing in the profile, the lesson player, for a connected module the digest of the current version and `skill_tiers` even before the first attempt — and the answers by which it erases on the device — the list of profiles, "account gone", version withdrawal by digest and the end of an attempt;
- the architecture of the first slice has no real-time channel, and no queues or event bus of its own either. Т-25 recommends no channel, and to return to it together with voice (Т-13), deciding then whether a channel is needed if voice input goes as a stream. canvas-api had a channel — Centrifugo —, and its author refused to remove it ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md)).

#### Common to all requests

**Common to all requests:**
- a request with a token carries the ID token of the sign-in service in `Authorization`, and `api` checks it with revocation and goes through the checks of sign-in and permissions in order;
- a learning request and an adult action on a profile carry the profile ID, even when the table below names only the attempt, the run or the choice: `api` first checks the right to the profile, and reads the attempt, the run and the choice by the pair "profile ID, their ID". Those of others and those that do not exist get one refusal — "not found";
- a request that calls AI carries the offset of the device's clock from UTC, and for an owner profile of the category `adult` with an own key, the key and its provider;
- an action that a repeat must not do twice carries an idempotency key — below;
- the wire is binary protobuf, and the server accepts JSON too — below.

**Requests.** "Key" is the idempotency key, "AI" means the request calls AI and carries the clock offset, and with an own key, the key and its provider instead of the offset.

**In the code**: `CheckOwnKey` checks the key by `Tutor.CheckKey`, through the gate's `CheckKey`, with no learner's content. `tutor` checks every model it would call with the key, at Anthropic by `count_tokens`, which is free (sources 10.7), and takes the worst end. A provider `OwnKeyOpen` closes is "provider closed" with no call, and so are OpenAI and a provider the table does not know. A key with no provider or no value is `invalid_argument` with no `Refusal` (Д245 spec), and a profile not of the category `adult` "key not for this profile", both with no call. A provider that gave no answer is `OWN_KEY_CHECK_UNSPECIFIED` with `CAUSE_AI_FAILURE`, an answer and not a refusal. The check ends a second before the request's deadline, and with less than two seconds left it does not begin: `deadline_exceeded`. The time of a check on the models is measurements 6. The key stays in the request's memory; the line `request` names the end alone, by `key_check`. On the stub of the dev mode, the ending of a synthetic key picks the end: `-invalid`, `-noperm`, `-silent`, or any other for "accepted".

**In the code**: no more than 10 checks of a key fit in any hour on the account. After the refusals above and before the gate, `countKeyCheck` holds the account's row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, drops the times of `accounts.key_checked_at` older than an hour, and with 10 left refuses `resource_exhausted` with `CAUSE_KEY_CHECKS_EXHAUSTED` and `next_key_check`, when the oldest of the 10 leaves the hour, with no call (Д247 spec); otherwise it adds this check's time and commits before the check, so that a check beside it waits and counts it. A provider closed counts as a check, so that trying providers does not get round the limit. On the stub of the dev mode there is no limit (`account.WithoutKeyCheckLimit`). The times go with the account's fields, and are no part of the export.

#### Refusals, outcomes, the idempotency key and what goes to the device

**Refusals and outcomes:**
- a refusal is an action that did not happen: a Connect error with a standard code and a cause with data. An outcome is an action that happened and changes what the learner sees: a field of the successful answer. An exhausted quota does not refuse a submission — the verdict "unchecked" with the cause "quota" —, but does not begin a new attempt or segment, and that is a refusal;
- what `app` does by the code: `unauthenticated` is a re-sign-in, that is, a sign-in of the same user, without signing out: signing out erases everything of the account on the device. `app` reads the cause before any refresh of the token. Without a cause, it refreshes the token and repeats the request, and opens the sign-in window only if the refresh failed; on "fresh sign-in needed" it signs in again by the window and repeats the action; on "account gone" it erases everything of the account and signs out, without refreshing the token; `unimplemented` is only an offer to reload: `api` no longer holds the major package of the tab; `unavailable` is a temporary refusal, and `app` repeats it with a growing pause, but not for long — a deployment takes minutes —, and then shows a failure; how long `app` decides; the rest `app` shows in its own words by the cause. **Idempotency key:**
- a field of the message, not a header: CORS allows only `Authorization` and the headers of Connect. `app` creates a key for each action of the learner and sends it with each repeat of that action;
- needed by the start of an attempt, a submission — of handwriting, of a choice and of an essay —, the words of a rung and the correct answer words, "explain differently", showing the solution and the request "something else";
- `api` writes the key at the start of the request, in the same transaction as the reserve of the quota or the action itself, together with the method, the target — an attempt, a run or, for "something else", the profile, and for a request of the account itself, the account — and the fingerprint of the request. The same key with another method, target or request is the refusal "key already used": otherwise the answer of another action would go as its own. A repeat while the first is going is the refusal "still going". A repeat after it is an answer by what is recorded, and a repeat does not call AI and does not spend the quota a second time:

| Request | A repeat with the same key gets |
|---|---|
| start of an attempt | the same attempt and its open view |
| submission of handwriting or of a choice | the recorded verdicts and what was read: the database holds the observation |
| essay submission | how the submission ended, without what was read and the words of the feedback: they are nowhere |
| words of a rung, "explain differently" | their words, while the attempt is open: the database holds them until the end of the attempt |
| correct answer words | an answer without words: they are nowhere. A lost answer is lost words, and this is an accepted price |
| showing the solution | the solution once more: the attempt is closed by the showing |
| "something else" | the same engine choice |
| finding the tasks of a page | an answer "repeated" without frames: the server keeps none |
| start of the attempts of a page | the same attempts |

A repeat of the deletion of a profile gets "no profile", a repeat of the deletion of the account gets "account gone", and `app` reads both as "deleted already". A second "lesson passed" is not an error, a repeat of "Done" sees the attempt closed "done" and answers as the first did, and a technique given at a step or on request is recorded, and a repeat gives the same one. Two simultaneous openings of a lesson are separated by a constraint of the database: the second request reads the open run again;

#### The wire, routes without a token, CORS, logs and the request timeout

**The wire:**
- binary protobuf. `app` turns it on in the transport connect-web — by default it sends JSON. A new value of an enumeration that a release of `spec` added to the package `v1` binary protobuf carries to an earlier build of `app` as a number: a proto3 enumeration is open. In JSON the server writes the value's name, and connect-es skips an unknown name, and the field reads as empty: a new close reason would look like "attempt open". Crops are `bytes`, in JSON a base64 string, a third longer, and the crop of a handwriting check goes twice ([connect-es v2.3.0](https://github.com/connectrpc/connect-es/tree/v2.3.0), [protobuf-es v2.16.0](https://github.com/bufbuild/protobuf-es/tree/v2.16.0));
- the server accepts JSON too, for debugging. The standard JSON codec of connect-go writes the names in lowerCamelCase, so `api` sets its own: `protojson` with `UseProtoNames`, as the rules of the contracts prescribe, and `DiscardUnknown`, as the standard one does ([connect-go v2.0.2](https://github.com/connectrpc/connect-go/blob/v2.0.2/connectproto/connectproto.go));
- the price: binary cannot be read by eye in the browser's tools.

**Routes without a token and withdrawal of a version:**
- the module manifest, the skill graph, the technique catalog and the solving steps are methods with the idempotency level `NO_SIDE_EFFECTS`, and `app` takes them by Connect GET, without `Connect-Timeout-Ms`: `app` sets no deadline on these calls, otherwise the header would make the request not simple, with a preflight for each digest. Such a request without headers goes without a preflight, is cached by HTTP, and carries no profile ID: the GET address holds the message, and it holds only the digest. Changing the level breaks compatibility for `buf breaking` `FILE`;
- the open view in the contract is the bytes of the format's JSON and beside them the format version: `api` and `app` read it by the format's types, and by the version `app` knows whether it will read the view, or offers to reload;
- the lesson and the task only with a token;
- a file of the device part is HTTP GET by the package digest and the file's path, without a token and headers, and `fetch` goes without a preflight and through the HTTP cache:

| Answer | When |
|---|---|
| `200` | the file |
| `404` | there is no such path in the device part of this package |
| `410` | the image no longer gives the version: it is withdrawn or its format version is out of the window |
| `503` with `Retry-After` | a digest that the image's index does not know: a deployment is going on, and a repeat reaches the new image |

- the HTTP cache of open views and files lasts a day: by digest they do not change, and a withdrawal reaches learners only with the author's pull request anyway, which the job "Withdrawals" of the module versions above reminds of. In the code: `Cache-Control: public, max-age=86400` on the answer `200`, for a file also `ETag` — the file's digest — and `304` by it, `no-store` on refusals, and the `503` of a file with `Retry-After: 30`. The file's type is by the extension of the four formats of `media/`, and `text/plain` for `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`, with `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. The open views are built once, at the start of `serve`, and a request of a file writes a row of the log `package_file`: the status and the time, without the address;
- withdrawal is a request of its own, "which of these digests does the image no longer give", from the account of the token, without a profile ID. `app` sends it at launch — with the digests whose open views it holds — and after the refusal "version withdrawn" or the answer `410`, and deletes their open views. The refusals stay, so that a withdrawal is seen in the middle of work too.

**CORS:** when `api` is on its own origin; on the origin of `app` it is not needed. The origin of `app` is the variable `API_APP_ORIGIN`, exact, without a path and `*`; when it is not set, there is no CORS:
- the exact origin of `app` from the configuration of `api`, on all paths and on all answers, errors and `410` and `503` too: otherwise `fetch` cannot tell a refusal from a network failure;
- the methods `GET` and `POST`; the headers `Authorization`, `Content-Type`, `Connect-Protocol-Version` and `Connect-Timeout-Ms`, and `Authorization` by name: `*` does not cover it; `Access-Control-Max-Age` is 7200 s; `Access-Control-Expose-Headers` is `Retry-After`: it is not in the safelist of response headers;
- the preflight cache is by the exact address: each POST method has its own, and Chromium holds it no longer than 2 hours and no more than 32 entries per top-level site ([98b9eab](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/98b9eab7ac9e36a7228c5143db756f699c8ec4e1/services/network/cors/preflight_result.cc), [`preflight_cache.h`](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/98b9eab7ac9e36a7228c5143db756f699c8ec4e1/services/network/cors/preflight_cache.h)), Firefox no longer than a day ([abbeff4](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/abbeff4fd9e02e3f6195153493fc0825eafe5d6d/netwerk/protocol/http/nsCORSListenerProxy.cpp)).

**Logs:** an interceptor of Connect writes the method, the code, the cause of a refusal and the time, the event `request`, and with it the class of a token refusal and, for a failure, the kind of error. It does not write the body, the headers or the address of the request: the body holds the work and the learner's data, a header holds the token, and the GET address holds the message. The request logs of Cloud Run are excluded.

**Request timeout** ([connect-go v2.0.2](https://github.com/connectrpc/connect-go/tree/v2.0.2)): 30 s, `API_REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, 90 s for `FindTasks`, `ReadStatement`, `SubmitWork`, `GetOpeningWords` and `AskTutor`, `API_PAPER_REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, and 300 s for `AskGrownUp`, `API_GROWN_UP_REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, each less than `API_HTTP_WRITE_TIMEOUT`, 330 s; shorter if the client's `Connect-Timeout-Ms` asks so. It governs the calls of the ports — the Firebase SDK repeats a call for up to two minutes —, the record "going" of the idempotency key and the finishing of marks by a job, as the database and learner model, above, and deletion and export, below, describe.

#### The real-time channel and its measurement

**Real-time channel** (Т-25): there is none. The check of handwriting is two requests, a tapped choice one, without AI, an essay one. How long the learner waits on Opus 5.5 · `high` is an estimate by the measurements of `tutor` ([be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/be607e5/docs/architecture.md)), not a measurement:

| What the learner waits for | Two requests — decided | One answer | Event channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| the verdict "not yet" | after reading, ≈ 3–4 s | after reading and words, ≈ 8–9 s | after reading, ≈ 3–4 s |
| the tutor's question | ≈ 5 s more and the overhead of the second request | together with the verdict | ≈ 5 s more |
| "correct" and the solution | after reading, ≈ 3–4 s; praise by the second request | after reading and praise | after reading |
| feedback on an essay | ≈ 12 s by one request | the same | the same |

- reading is the pair of readers of one provider. The estimate takes the time of the judge with the key on the board, the same call with the picture of the work: for Opus 5.5 · `high` p50 2.5–3.9 s, for Haiku 5.5 · `low` a median of 2.7 s; the documents of `tutor` give no time of a reading call ([be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/be607e5/docs/architecture.md)). The numbers are for readers that go in parallel, and the pair takes the time of Opus; one after the other, ≈ 2–3 s more to the verdict. The estimate is for Haiku 5.5 · `low`, not for the effort the pair of `tutor` takes from its list `admission.Admitted`, which the platform AI quota above prices;
- words are the analysis and the hint, Opus 5.5 · `high`, p50 5.1 s ([`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/be607e5/docs/architecture.md));
- an essay is reading and assessment, Opus 5.5 · `high`, p50 11.9 s ([`tutor` at be607e5](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/be607e5/docs/architecture.md));
- the overhead of the second request: the check of the token with revocation, by a network call to the sign-in service; a preflight, if `api` is on its own origin and the cache entry for the method has expired; the crop once more, by binary protobuf, without base64. On the first app the second request without a call, `GetRungWords` with a fallback text, took about a second (measurements 2.1);
- a channel does not speed up the verdict: the answer of the first request is the early verdict. It would win only the overhead of the second request, and itself would need a service of its own and a subscription by token;
- the first app works locally: `app` and `api` on one machine, the preflight local, and the check of the token, the database and the AI provider over the network, as in a deployment.

**Measurement on the first app:** measurements 2.1 and 2.2. The verdict came later than the estimate, and the words took twice their estimate of ≈ 5 s. A channel would give the verdict no earlier, and the words earlier only by the overhead of the second request, and the verdict does not wait for them. Why reading is longer than the judge of `tutor`, and the words longer than the measurement of `tutor`, is Д222 spec.

**Against canvas-api**, which kept the channel and rejected «всё в HTTP-ответе» ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md)): the tutor's words go whole, after the leak check, so a channel would carry only events; the verdict is ready after the first call, the reading, so a channel would carry one event, the one that comes as an answer anyway; the rules of the contracts do not allow streams; there are no queues or event bus of our own. The argument of canvas-api itself — on Cloud Run work after the response gets almost no CPU — holds ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md)): each request does its work within itself. Return to the channel together with voice (Т-13), at stage 3, and decide then whether it is needed, if voice input goes as a stream.

#### The check against privacy

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- the fields of requests that carry learners' data — the profile's fields, the clock offset, the work and the profile ID — are those that the privacy document of `spec` names. The work and the own key live only in the request's memory, and the profile's fields and the offset on the server, as in the table of what the database stores;
- the record of the idempotency key is a service record, without content, for no longer than 2 days, and it does not go into the export; `api` does not store the digests of the request about withdrawals;
- what goes to AI from the requests is what the privacy document of `spec` names: the work, and of the presentation style only what changes the words. The voicing, the mode and the setting of showing do not go to AI;
- GET and files without a token carry only the digest and the path, the content of modules, not a learner's data;
- logs without the body, the headers and the address;
- the promise of the privacy document of `spec` "What the learner writes by hand stays on the device" holds: the crop goes to the check and lives in the request's memory, and a repeat with a key does not store it. A work reaches the server only by `PutWork`, while whoever consents for the learner keeps its storage on, and goes to no AI provider;
- the adult's progress shows what the privacy document of `spec` already holds on the server: attempts, marks and traps, and from the closed trap catalog it gets the `description` and `trap_advice` of a repeating trap, as the text of the module format recorded.

### 3.10. Deletion and export

This section holds the fresh sign-in, the class of a token check refusal and the answers "fresh sign-in needed" and "account gone"; the deletion of a profile and of the account, the operator's job, consent, honest periods and the export. The facts about Firebase, RFC 9470, Connect, Cloud Run jobs and the size of a response of Cloud Run are in the records sources 11.1–11.3, 11.6 and 11.8, and the code of the Firebase SDKs and of OpenFGA, canvas-api and `app` are links beside the claims.

**What the decision rests on:**
- deletion is confirmed by a fresh sign-in, not by the PIN. `api` deletes the data from the database and the account at the sign-in service, and the other devices erase their data at their next call to `api`: it answers that the account is gone. The honest periods name the backups of the database, the sign-in service, the logs of the platform and of the AI providers, the other devices and the record of consent, and what remains of consent is the minimal record below;
- one account holds both the owner profile and the profiles of their children, and the export and deletion by profile and of the whole account are detailed by `api`;
- П-14 is closed: a minimal record of a child's consent — the date and the method of the check, without the child's data — is kept 5 years after the deletion of the account or the profile and erases itself; the platform has no lawyer;
- the Admin SDK for Go holds the time of sign-in in the field `AuthTime` of the token. `VerifyIDTokenAndCheckRevoked` does not give the token on a refusal, and names a deleted user `IsUserNotFound`, which `IsIDTokenInvalid` does not cover. An ID token lives up to an hour after revocation, and revocation is precise to a second ([firebase-admin-go v4.22.0](https://github.com/firebase/firebase-admin-go/tree/v4.22.0));
- in the Firebase SDK for the web, refreshing the token of a deleted user ends with `auth/user-token-expired`, the same code as for a revoked sign-in, and the SDK signs out by itself. `getIdToken(true)` does not change `auth_time`, and a re-sign-in by the window requires the same UID ([`@firebase/auth` 1.13.8](https://github.com/firebase/firebase-js-sdk/tree/@firebase/auth@1.13.8/packages/auth));
- in canvas-api a sign-in no older than 5 minutes was fresh, by `auth_time`, and it was required by the deletion of a child's profile, the deletion of the account and the change of a forgotten PIN, and the PIN was a barrier of the interface, not a security boundary. Tokens there were checked with revocation, so that an old token from another device would not create the parent's record anew ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs));
- canvas-api deleted the database, then the tuples, then `RevokeRefreshTokens` and `DeleteUser`, and only the client led a repeat: a failure after the database left the Firebase user without a period, and the other devices got no answer ([0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/tree/0901af0/docs));
- `app` erases on the device at sign-out everything of the account, at the sign-in of another account everything of the previous one, a profile deleted on another device by the list of profiles at launch, and the account by "account gone" ([375a09b](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/blob/375a09b/docs/architecture.md)).

**Fresh sign-in:**
- the `auth_time` of a token that passed the check is no older than 300 s by the clock of `api`. An `auth_time` ahead of the clock of `api` by no more than 300 s is fresh too: this is the same tolerance that the Admin SDK gives the token's expiry;
- it is required by the deletion of a profile, the deletion of the account and the change of a forgotten PIN, and it is checked last, after the right. The export and the other adult actions do not require it, and the PIN is not a confirmation;
- otherwise, the refusal "fresh sign-in needed", `unauthenticated`, and in the data the allowed age of the sign-in in seconds, as `max_age` of RFC 9470. `app` signs in again by the window and repeats the action;
- how `app` refreshes the sign-in `app` decides; the contract asks it: compare `authTime` with the `issuedAtTime` of the token just refreshed, not with the device's clock; sign in again by the window on a press; on `auth/user-mismatch` — the SDK gives the same code to a deleted user — ask `api`.

**Class of a token check refusal.** The port of the sign-in service gives the UID, `auth_time` and the class of refusal, and does not wrap the errors of the SDK before the helpers `Is…`: they compare the type.

| Refusal of `VerifyIDTokenAndCheckRevoked` | What `api` does | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| `IsIDTokenExpired` | — | `unauthenticated` without a cause: the SDK will refresh the token |
| `IsIDTokenRevoked` | reads the UID by `VerifyIDToken`: the signature by Google's keys from the cache, without `GetUser` | the account has the mark "being deleted": "account gone"; no mark: `unauthenticated` without a cause |
| `IsUserNotFound` | reads the UID by `VerifyIDToken`, the same way | "account gone". If the UID has an account without the mark — the user was deleted bypassing `api`, for example in the project's console —, `api` deletes the account by the same route as below, without a fresh sign-in and in this same request: step 1, and step 3 is done already. What did not pass the operator's job finishes |
| `IsUserDisabled` | — | `unauthenticated` without a cause: `api` does not disable users, and the SDK signs out by itself with `auth/user-disabled` |
| `IsIDTokenInvalid` — broken, empty, of another project or signed not by a Google key | — | "invalid": `unauthenticated` without a cause, as for an expired one |
| a failure: Google's keys that cannot be got, the network, a 5xx answer of the sign-in service | — | `unavailable` |

A token without a refusal goes on: for a marked account any request, the creation of the account and the redemption of a code too, gets "account gone", and the others go through the seven steps of the checks of sign-in and permissions. A UID without an account whose user is alive at the sign-in service gets "no access". If the sign-in service has no such user — the job or the console deleted them, and the token still lives —, this is "account gone": an account could not be created for such a UID.

**Deletion of a profile:** separately from the account, by `can_manage` and a fresh sign-in. The owner profile is deleted the same way, and the last profile does not delete the account: the account holds the admission, the tariff and the counters.
1. One transaction of the database of `api`. The account's row `FOR KEY SHARE`, with a check of the mark, the profile's row `FOR UPDATE`. Everything about the profile is deleted: the attempts, the observations, the words of hints, the submissions with a rubric, the lesson runs, the evidence log, the state cache, the engine choices, the connected modules, the profile's counters, the idempotency keys and the profile itself. The child consent becomes a minimal record, an adult's consent is deleted whole, and the account's counters stay.
2. After `COMMIT`, the tuple of OpenFGA. If it was not deleted, it is "dangling": there is no row of the profile, and a request by it gets "no profile", and the daily job removes it.
3. The answer "deleted". `app` erases the profile's database on this device, and the other devices by the list of profiles.

A repeat at which the tuple is there and the row is not deletes the tuple and answers "no profile", the same refusal as the profile's step of the checks of sign-in and permissions. `app` reads it on deletion as "deleted already".

**Deletion of the account:** by the account from the token and a fresh sign-in:
1. One transaction of the database of `api`:
   - the account's row `FOR UPDATE`, and on it the mark "being deleted" with the time;
   - everything about each profile, as in step 1 above;
   - the child consents into minimal records, the adults' consents and the "waiting" consents without a profile whole;
   - the account's counters and the account's fields: the owner's statement, the PIN hash, its counter and lock, the times of the key checks, the tariff, the offset, the start and the end of the day, the date of the last request;
   - the binding of the invite code is removed, and the code stays redeemed: SHA-256 without a binding is not personal data, and a new account needs a new code.

   The account's row keeps the ID, the UID of the sign-in service and the time of the mark.
2. The tuples of all profiles: `api` reads them by `user:<account ID>` and deletes them in batches, and skips the missing ones.
3. `RevokeRefreshTokens`, then `DeleteUser`; "no such user" means that the step is done already. The step goes even if step 2 did not pass: the sign-in service holds the identity data, for up to 180 days after deletion too, and the tuples hold only IDs. The step is done when `DeleteUser` passed: if the revocation did not pass, `DeleteUser` goes anyway, since a refresh token stops working also when "The user is deleted", and the check with revocation answers "no such user".
4. Only when steps 2 and 3 have passed, a transaction: the cleanup of step 1 once more, for the rows that were written late, and the account's row itself.
5. The answer "deleted". `app` erases everything of the account and signs out.

If steps 2–4 did not pass, the answer is "deleted" anyway: there is nothing about the learners in the database, and the operator's job finishes the rest. A repeat after a lost answer gets "account gone", and `app` does the same.

**Where it is in the code.** Deletion is the package `internal/deletion`: `Profile`, `Account` and `Finish`, which is steps 2–4 for a marked account, called by `Account` and the operator's job. It is called by `DeleteProfile` and `DeleteAccount` of the package `internal/account` and by the interceptor, on the route "no such user". What did not pass after the database is "unfinished": the answer "deleted" with the code `ok`, and the row `request` of the log an error with the kind of failure. A dangling tuple on a repeat of `DeleteProfile` is deleted by the interceptor: there is no row of the profile under the account, and the right is there.

**Races:**
- each write transaction under an account takes its row `FOR KEY SHARE` and checks the mark, and the deletion of the account takes it `FOR UPDATE`. A request that passed the checks before the mark creates under a marked account neither a profile, nor a consent, nor a PIN, nor a counter. The lock is `FOR KEY SHARE`, not `FOR SHARE`: a request that then updates the account's row itself — the date of the request, the PIN counter — would with `FOR SHARE` fall into a deadlock with a neighbouring request of the same account, and `FOR KEY SHARE` does not hinder such an update and still conflicts with the `FOR UPDATE` of deletion;
- a request that holds a profile's consent and then changes the profile holds the profile's row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` first, as the deletion holds it before its cascade reaches the consent: they wait, not deadlock, and the one after answers "no profile";
- the operator's command that confirms a consent rejects a marked account;
- a row about a profile that is written after its deletion fails on the foreign key, and an `UPDATE` that did not find the profile's row answers "no profile";
- the quota reserve of a deleted profile is counted only to the account's counter;
- two token checks with revocation in one second: a token issued in the same second the Admin SDK does not count as revoked. The next check, after `DeleteUser`, will name it deleted, and before it the request sees the mark.

**The operator's job** is the daily job of retention periods. It also closes abandoned attempts and lesson runs, deletes the quota counters and the records of idempotency keys after their period and backs up the closing of attempts on a withdrawn version and out of the window, as showing the solution, the lesson, the platform AI quota, the protocol and module versions above describe. Deletion adds this to it:
- finishes the marks "being deleted" older than the longer request timeout, 90 s: steps 2–4 of the deletion of the account;
- deletes by period by the same route, only without a fresh sign-in, and checks the period under the lock of the row: a profile after 12 months without activity, an account after 12 months without requests (П-9). Otherwise an owner who came back on the same day would lose the data;
- deletes the users of the sign-in service without an account of `api` who have not signed in or refreshed the token for 30 days. The iteration is `Users` of the Admin SDK, the time is `LastLogInTimestamp` and `LastRefreshTimestamp` of its `UserMetadata`, and with them `CreationTimestamp`: the period runs from the latest of the three, so a user who was created and has not yet signed in is kept 30 days from creation. The account is checked right before deletion, for each user. Such a user appears for everyone who signed in without an invite code, and for one who signed in again after deletion: the sign-in service gives them a new UID. Without the job nobody would delete them, and the 180 days of Firebase begin only with deletion; П-9 and the row "Account" of the privacy document of `spec` name the period;
- cleans the "dangling" tuples and the `changelog` of OpenFGA, as the database and learner model above describe.

A period that depends on the job is "until the next successful pass of the job". In the first app the job is an operator's command that the author runs, as sign-in and permissions describe.

**Where it is in the code.** The job is the command `api sweep`, the package `internal/sweep`: one pass per run, the steps of `logging.Steps` in their order — among them the marks, the accounts and profiles by period, the users of the sign-in service without an account, the dangling tuples, the minimal records of child consents older than 5 years, the records of keys and `changelog`. There is no state between passes: each step chooses its work anew by a query, so the next pass picks up an interrupted one. A pass goes no longer than the flag `-for`, 9 minutes by default, and stops between elements (sources 11.6). The period of 12 months is checked under the lock of the row: `deletion.IdleAccount` and `deletion.IdleProfile`, by the same route as deletion on request. A dangling tuple is one older than the request timeout and without a row of the profile at its account, and a user without an account is one 30 days after the latest of the sign-in, the token refresh and the creation. The log is a row `swept` per step, with the counters, and the exit with code 0 comes only after a clean pass. In a deployment the command is run by a Cloud Run job on a schedule that `infra` gives; there it is recognized by `CLOUD_RUN_JOB`, and it has no service account key.

**Consent.** A minimal record is only for a child consent:
- the ID of the consent record, the date, the method of the check and the date of deletion. There is neither the account's and the profile's ID, nor the pseudonym, nor the age category, nor the country in it;
- it can be linked to the family only through the export file at the owner, where the consent lies whole, with the record's ID, and through the scan of the signed form at the operator, who holds it the same period and then erases it;
- the period is 5 years after the deletion of the account or the profile, by the calendar: an older record is erased by the daily job `api sweep` by the step `consent_records`;
- an adult's consent is deleted whole: it has no signed form, and there would be nothing to check the record against.

The operator confirms a child's consent by `api consents`, and the withdrawal of one's own consent deletes the profile, as sign-in and permissions describe. The record is the row "Consent" of the privacy document of `spec`, with its period.

**What stays where, and for how long**, after the deletion of a profile or of the account:

| Where | What stays | For how long |
|---|---|---|
| the database of `api` | the minimal records of child consents | 5 years after deletion |
| the database of `api` | the account's row with the mark "being deleted": the ID, the UID and the time | seconds; on a failure, until the next successful pass of the job |
| the database `openfga` | a "dangling" tuple, if it could not be deleted | until the next pass of the job |
| the `changelog` of OpenFGA | the records about the deleted tuples | no longer than 2 days, as the database above keeps it |
| the history of Neon, both databases | everything deleted, as it was | the history window after each deletion: 1 day in the deployment, 6 hours in the dev project |
| the sign-in service, Firebase Auth | the user | deleted at once, on a failure until the next successful pass of the job; after that Firebase holds the data up to 180 days, and the IP addresses a few weeks (П-9) |
| the sign-in service, Firebase Auth | a user who signed in again after deletion or without an invite code | 30 days without a sign-in and a token refresh, then the same 180 days |
| the logs of `api` | nothing: there is neither an ID nor content in them | — |
| the request logs of Cloud Run | nothing: they are excluded | — |
| the logs of the hosting of `app` | IP addresses | their period is П-9, the hosting is chosen by `infra` (Т-17) |
| AI providers | only what went into a call, without the learner's and the account's ID | by the platform's contract, and with an own key by the terms that the key's owner accepted |
| eval samples | works given by a separate consent | outside the platform and its deletion (П-10) |
| payment | — | from stage 3 (О-14) |

| other devices | the data of the account or the profile | at the next request to `api` within an hour, otherwise at the next sign-in on the device; if it is not opened, until the browser or an adult erases it |
| the export file | everything exported | at the account owner, and they delete it |
| the account's counters, at the deletion of a profile | the counters of the day | no longer than 2 days after the end of their day |

| the link "Sign in with Google" in the owner's Google account | the link to the app | the deletion of the user at the sign-in service does not remove it. `app` is to remove it by revoking the Google token at deletion (Д221 spec), and until then the owner removes it themselves, on Google's page of connected apps |

**Other devices:**
- while their ID token lives — about an hour after `DeleteUser`, and the Admin SDK accepts it 5 more minutes after expiry —, `api` answers "account gone", and `app` erases everything of the account and signs out;
- later the Firebase SDK signs out by itself, at a token refresh and at the launch of `app`, with `auth/user-token-expired`, and the device does not reach `api`. The account's data stay on the device until someone signs in on it again. A new sign-in with the same Google account gives a new UID, which has no account, and `api` answers "no access"; "no access" while the registry of `app` holds an account is also "the sign-in of another account", and `app` erases the previous one;
- a device that is no longer opened holds the data until the browser or an adult erases them;
- a profile deleted on another device `app` erases by the list of profiles at launch, and to a request with its ID `api` answers "no profile".

**Export:**
- of a profile by `can_manage`, of the account by the account from the token, without a fresh sign-in;
- the content is the column "in the export" of the table of what the database stores. For an attempt, the close reason, for a lesson's task, the run ID and the segment place, for a child's profile, "ask before showing"; the lesson runs and the submissions with a rubric; the counters, the profile's in the profile's export, the account's in the account's export; the consents whole and the date of the last request;
- without secrets and service data: the UID, the PIN hash, its counter and lock, the times of the key checks, the binding of the code, the seed, the idempotency keys and the tuples. There is no own key on the server, and none in the export either;
- the format is the part `export/` of `spec`, below. "Export and delete in one step", as the privacy document of `spec` promises, is at `app`: a fresh sign-in, the export, the deletion.

**Check against the privacy document of `spec`:**
- honest periods: the table above names the backups, the logs, the other devices, the sign-in service, the AI providers and the record of consent. There is no promise "nothing will remain anywhere";
- the account owner manages the data: they export and delete, for a child a parent or a guardian, the profile and the account separately;
- deletion covers each place where the learner's and the owner's data lie: the database of `api`, the store of OpenFGA, the sign-in service and the device. What remains of consent is the minimal record of the row "Consent", for 5 years;
- neither IDs nor the adult's data go to AI, so at the providers there is nothing to delete by account, and what they hold from a call is their terms: the processor agreements are part of the providers' terms, and the platform asked both for zero data retention. The own key lives only in the request's memory;
- photos from paper and artifacts lie on the device and are erased by the rules of `app`. A stored work is on the server only while its storage is on: switching off erases it in its transaction, and the deletion of a profile or of the account by the cascade;
- "data can be exported and deleted in one step" and "we honestly name the periods of deletion" hold: this is the fresh sign-in, the export and the deletion at `app` and the table above;
- the periods of the table above follow П-9, and the 5 years of the record of consent are the period the platform sets for it.

**In the code**, the export: `ExportProfile`, by an adult action or by a learner with their own consent, and `ExportAccount` give `api.zip` by the part `export/` of `spec`, the package `internal/export`: its version 5, `exportv5`, whose profile's file names the stored works, newest first, as `ListWorks` does, which `app` reads each by `GetWork`, the open attempts of a page from paper, with nothing of their Check, and an adult's correction of a level by its `correction_id`. The evidence, the state and the observations are protobuf-JSON by the `.proto` of the release file of `learner-model` accepted by `release-verify` (`just learner-model-proto`), and `observation.proto` of the contracts. The withdrawal of one's own consent deletes the profile, as its deletion does, and leaves the minimal record and the problem reports without an ID.

**What the code decided**: what the design left to it, because it can be seen only in the code:
